 Reciprocal employment – Academic exchanges [R205(b)-C22] – (exemption code C22)- Canadian interests – International Mobility Program

This section contains policy, procedures and guidance used by IRCC staff. It is posted on the department’s website as a courtesy to stakeholders.

In these instructions “officer” refers to employees of both Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada and the Canada Border Services Agency.

The instructions on this page should be reviewed in conjunction with

  • Employer-specific work permits – General processing – International Mobility Program
  • Assessing the genuineness of the offer of employment on a work permit application – subsection 200(5) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations (IRPR)
  • Conditions and validity period on work permits
  • Public list of Employers who have been non-compliant
  • Reciprocal employment general guidelines [R205(b) – C20] – Canadian interests – International Mobility Program

Paragraph R205(b) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations (IRPR) allows temporary workers to take up employment in Canada when these opportunities also create or maintain reciprocal employment in other countries for Canadian citizens or permanent residents of Canada.

This includes exchanges between Canada and the country of residence or citizenship of the foreign worker, or employment opportunities between Canada and other locations that the employer (such as a multinational company or academic institution) may offer. Note that other qualifications, such as licensing and medical requirements (if applicable), must still be met.

Academia is a field where exchanges and mobility are very common, especially among faculty, teaching and research staff and at the post-graduate level.

Note: Post-doctoral fellows and award recipients are facilitated under R205(c)(ii), C44).

Strict job-for-job reciprocity is not necessarily required to be considered for facilitation under this category.

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada recognizes that opportunities exist for Canadians to take similar positions in foreign academic institutions, and therefore allows for the application of R205(b), administrative code C22 for guest lecturers,Footnote 1 teachers (elementary and secondary), and visiting professors.

Place of application

R200(1)(a) requires that the application be submitted in accordance with R197 (outside Canada), R198, (at the port of entry) or R199 (inside Canada).

For more information on the requirements of R198, consult Persons who may apply at a port of entry and for R199 consult Persons who may apply from within Canada.

General eligibility

To be eligible under R205(b), administrative code C22, a foreign national must

  • have an offer of employment submitted as per R209.11 by an educational institution or school board of employment that outlines the role the foreign national will fulfill [such as guest lecturer, teacher (elementary or secondary), or visiting professor].

Specific eligibility – Guest lecturers

Guest lecturers may be eligible under R205(b) for a C22 work permit only if they

  • are invited by a post-secondary institutionFootnote 2 to give a series of lectures; and
  • will occupy a temporary position of a non-continuing nature (which does not include a complete academic course) for a period of less than 1 academic term or semester.

Specific eligibility – Teachers (elementary and secondary)

Teachers may be eligible under R205(b) for a C22 work permit only if they

  • are hired by an educational institution as elementary and secondary teachers; and
  • are coming to Canada under reciprocal exchange agreements arranged between foreign academic authorities and Canadian provincial/territorial governments or school boards, whose schools follow a curriculum/program of studies approved by a provincial/territorial government.

Note: Work permit duration will be based on the offer of employment.

A letter from the relevant educational institution or school board of employment must be included with the work permit application and contain

  • information explaining the exchange agreement in place (including the title or name of agreement the worker is participating in) or a copy of the exchange agreement under which the individual is being hired
  • a description of the recentFootnote* opportunities for Canadians in the sending institution or country of the reciprocal exchange agreement
  • signature and the date

Specific eligibility – Visiting professors

Visiting professors may be eligible under R205(b) for a C22 work permit if they

  • are working for a period of not more than 2 academic years;
  • are taking a position with a post-secondary institution; and
  • will retain their position abroad.

Visiting professors may also include those on sabbatical who are doing collaborative research with a Canadian post-secondary institution.

Documentary evidence

The onus is on the applicant to provide evidence that they meet the eligibility requirements of administrative code C22 and will be able to perform the work sought as stated in the offer of employment.

With the application for a work permit, the following documentary evidence to make an assessment must be provided:

  • evidence that the employer has submitted an International Mobility Program (IMP) offer of employment through the Employer Portal or that alternative submission has been authorized

Note: Teachers (elementary and secondary) must also provide evidence (e.g. a letter) that indicates reciprocal international opportunities exist for Canadian citizens or permanent residents of Canada in the sending institution or country.

Application assessment

When officers are reviewing the work permit application, the following factors should be reviewed:

Immigration Medical Exam (IME)

Elementary and secondary teachers require a medical examination as they are designated occupations.

Guest lecturers or visiting professors require a medical examination if they will be working in a setting in which the protection of the public health is essential (healthcare, childcare, etc.) or if they will be in Canada for 6 months or more and have resided in or travelled to 1 or more of these countries or territories for 6 consecutive months or more in a row in the year before applying. For further information Medical exams for visitors, students and workers.

Offer of employment submitted through the Employer Portal

The employer must submit an offer of employment, as per section R209.11, before a work permit application is made.

Important note: The employer is subject to regulatory imposed conditions based on the information provided in the offer of employment. Therefore, any corrections to the information in the form must come from the employer.

Specifically, if the employer has made an error in the offer of employment, officers should not just correct the information in Global Case Management System (GCMS). Instead, they may

  • refuse the application based on the foreign national not meeting the eligibility requirements, if applicable; or
  • contact the employer and instruct them to submit a new offer of employment.

When assessing if the work permit application meets the requirements of R205(b) for academic exchanges, officers should review the offer of employment that appears under the Employment Details tab in GCMS for information provided by the employer or the matching fields on the IMM 5802 form (if employer authorized to use it).

Duration

Officers may issue a work permit that is valid for the duration of the offer of employment or until the expiry of the travel document, whichever is earlier. If the foreign national is exempt from the travel document requirement (for example, they are a United States citizen), the work permit should be issued for the full duration of the offer of employment.

Refer to Validity period for work permits.
Conditions (Mandatory when no medical exam is completed)

Officers should ensure that instructions provided in Open work permit issuance in GCMS: Occupation or location restricted are followed.

Applicants with valid IME results do not require a condition on their work permit.

If a medical examination was not completed, 1 of the conditions below must be imposed. The specific occupational sector restriction will depend on whether the person has resided in a designated or non-designated country.

  • For persons who have not resided in a designated country:

    “Not authorized to work in 1) child care, 2) primary or secondary school teaching, 3) health services field occupations.”

  • For persons who have resided in a designated country:

    “Not authorized to work in 1) child care, 2) primary or secondary school teaching, 3) health services field occupations, 4) agricultural occupations.”
Fees

Work permit processing fee: $155

Employer compliance fee: $230

Biometric fee (if applicable): $85
Biometrics

Work permit applicants are required to provide their biometrics.

The regular biometric exemptions apply (for example, under 14 years or 1 in 10 rule).

Refusal

If an officer is not satisfied that all the requirements of R200, including the assessment under R205(b) are met, they must record their reasons and outline the rationale underlying the decision as well as the facts and elements considered. They must also provide an explanation for the decision in a case note.

Refusal reasons should clearly indicate which criteria or what requirement of R200 or R205(b) was not met and explain how the conclusion was reached. For assistance, officers can follow the steps in Decision making: Standard of review and process for making a reasonable decision.
