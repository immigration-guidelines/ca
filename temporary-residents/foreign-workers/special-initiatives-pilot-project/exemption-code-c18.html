Atlantic Immigration Program (AIP) [R204(c) – C18] – Agreements or arrangements – International Mobility Program (IMP)

This section contains policy, procedures and guidance used by IRCC staff. It is posted on the department’s website as a courtesy to stakeholders.

These instructions apply to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) employees. Given that applicants cannot submit their application at a port of entry, the instructions do not apply to border services officers.

The instructions on this page should be reviewed in conjunction with the following:

  • Employer-specific work permits – General processing – International Mobility Program 
  • Assessing the genuineness of the offer of employment on a work permit application – R200(5)
  • Conditions and validity period on work permits
  • Public list of Employers who have been non-compliant
  • Atlantic Immigration Program (AIP): Assessing the application against selection criteria

The Atlantic Immigration Class, referred to as the Atlantic Immigration Program (AIP), was established through regulations on January 1, 2022. The goal of this program is to attract and retain skilled foreign workers and international graduates in Atlantic Canada through an employer-driven approach. The AIP replaced the temporary Atlantic Immigration Pilot Program (AIPP), which expired on December 31, 2021.

A foreign national who is a member of the Atlantic Immigration Class as per section 87.3 of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations (IRPR) and who has received an endorsement by an Atlantic province as a candidate for permanent residence (PR) under an Atlantic immigration agreement between that province and the Minister may meet the requirements of paragraph R204(c) under administrative code C18.

Facilitating entry, or enabling workers to remain in Canada if they are already here, under the International Mobility Program (IMP) supports paragraph 3(1)(e) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (IRPA) by encouraging the settlement and retention of workers in the Atlantic region. Issuance of work permits to clients who are pursuing PR in the region allows them to fill labour market needs and supports their integration in the community.

Eligibility

To be eligible for a work permit under the AIP, paragraph R204(c) – administrative code C18, the foreign national must

  • submit their work permit application online
    • an application made under the AIP at a port of entry is not eligible
  • have an offer of employment from a designated employer under the AIP that was made in the Employer Portal (or using alternate means if authorized to do so) and includes the employer compliance fee
  • have a work permit support letter (WPSL) from the Atlantic province where the applicant will be working, issued as per the agreement (see Support letter from the endorsing Atlantic province)
  • demonstrate their proficiency in an official language according to program requirements
  • have a qualifying educational credential
  • meet the work experience requirements or meet the recent graduate requirements for the AIP

Important: The applicant does not need to have applied for PR before submitting an initial work permit application if the endorsement certificate is still valid (that is, if the work permit application is submitted prior to the expiration of the endorsement certificate). Note that the endorsement certificate expiry is indicated in the WPSL issued by the province.

Additional eligibility criteria – International graduates

To be eligible as an international graduate of an institute in an endorsing Atlantic province, the applicant must have, in addition to the above criteria, all of the following:

  • lived in New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Newfoundland and Labrador, or Prince Edward Island for at least 16 months (cumulative) within the 24-month period before their credential was granted
  • had valid temporary resident status and authorization for any work, study or training they engaged in while in Canada
  • a qualifying educational credential that meets the recent graduate requirements

Eligible international graduates are not required to meet work experience requirements.  

Specific eligibility criteria – Work permit renewals

Foreign nationals, including those who hold work permits in different streams, may apply to renew their work permit under the AIP as long as either

  • the endorsement is still valid (a work permit is submitted prior to the expiration date on the certificate), or
  • an application for PR was submitted prior to the expiration date on the certificate.

AIP applicants continue to be eligible to renew their work permit until a decision is made on their PR application. The maximum validity for each work permit is 2 years, but successive work permits may be issued as long as the PR application is pending and has not been refused. This includes AIPP applicants whose PR application is still pending.

Note that renewal applications require a new offer of employment to be made in the Employer Portal, including payment of the employer compliance fee.

Note: Applicants under either the AIP or the AIPP are not eligible for a bridging open work permit (administrative code A75) as their eligibility for the program is based on them receiving an offer of employment from a designated employer.

Documentary evidence

When an application for a work permit is made for the first time under the AIP, officers should be satisfied that they have the following documentary evidence to make an assessment:

  • a complete work permit application submitted electronically, including application fees
  • a WPSL issued by the endorsing Atlantic province requesting that IRCC issue a work permit (see Support letter from the endorsing Atlantic province)
    • if the WPSL indicates that the endorsement certificate has expired, an application for PR must have been submitted prior to the expiration date, in which case the officer must be satisfied that they have
      • if the applicant is outside Canada
        • a copy of the acknowledgment of receipt (AOR)
      • if the applicant is in-Canada
        • a copy of the AOR, or
        • a copy of the email confirming that the application for permanent residence (APR) was submitted in the online portal and proof of payment of the application fees, or
        • confirmation in the IRCC system that an APR was received and is still pending (includes prospective applications)
  • a completed and signed Undertaking for an application for a work permit exempted from a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) as part of the Atlantic Immigration Program (AIP) (IMM 0156E [PDF, 256 KB]) form
  • an offer of employment number (LMIA exemption number) generated when a designated employer under the AIP submits the offer of employment in the Employer Portal, or an offer of employment made through an alternate submission, if authorized
    • the information in the offer of employment should match the information provided in the WPSL
  • proof that the foreign national is able to perform the work sought
  • qualifying language test results that are less than 2 years old (or if the applicant has already made an APR, results that were less than 2 years old at the time the APR was submitted)
  • skilled workers (not described under the Additional eligibility criteria – International graduates section) must also provide
    • proof of Qualifying work experience, such as letter(s) of employment
    • proof of a qualifying educational credential for skilled workers
      • If the applicant was educated outside Canada, an educational credential assessment (ECA) that is less than 5 years old must be provided.

Additional documentary evidence – International graduates

International graduates of an institute in an endorsing Atlantic province must also provide

  • proof of residence in 1 of the Atlantic provinces for at least 16 months during the last 2 years before graduation, such as
    • copies of a lease or rental agreement, a letter from a landlord or property owner attesting to residence and specifying dates, utility bills that contain the applicant’s residential address, or other reasonable evidence that satisfies the officer
  • proof of valid temporary resident status and authorization for any periods of work or study completed during their stay
  • proof of a qualifying educational credential from a recognized Atlantic post-secondary institution that meets the recent graduate requirements

Specific documentary evidence – Work permit renewals

Renewing when previous permit was under the AIP

Applicants that have already had a work permit issued under the AIP and are applying for a renewal must provide

  • a new offer of employment number
  • if the endorsement certificate has expired, proof that they submitted an APR prior to the certificate expiration date, such as
    • a copy of the AOR
    • a copy of the email confirming that the APR was submitted in the online portal and proof of payment of the application fees
    • confirmation in the IRCC system that an APR was received and is still pending (includes prospective applications)
  • if there is a change of employer, an updated WPSL

Applicants renewing a work permit that was previously issued under administrative code C18 do not have to submit the following supporting documents with their renewal application:

  • a WPSL issued by the endorsing Atlantic province, unless there is a change of employer
  • a completed IMM 0156 form, unless there is a change of employer
  • educational credentials
  • language test results
  • proof of qualifying work experience
  • any of the additional documentary evidence for international graduates

Renewing when previous permit was under a different category

Foreign workers that are renewing their work permit but applying under the AIP for the first time are subject to the same documentary evidence as first-time AIP applicants.

As long as the endorsement certificate is still valid (WP or APR submitted prior to the expiration of the certificate), they may apply for a work permit under administrative code C18. If the WPSL indicates that the endorsement certificate date has expired, the applicant must provide proof that they submitted their APR so the officer can confirm that the APR was submitted before the expiration of the certificate.

Support letter from the endorsing Atlantic province

Applicants under the AIP must include a work permit support letter (WPSL), issued by the endorsing Atlantic province, with their work permit application. The WPSL should include the following information:

  • biographical information for the applicant
  • employer name, address and contact information
  • occupation title and National Occupational Classification (NOC) code
  • an employment start date
  • a request for IRCC to issue an employer-specific work permit
  • a statement that the province has determined
    • the employer urgently needs the applicant to start work before the APR is complete
    • the job offer is consistent with the requirements of the regulations
    • the employment is not part-time or seasonal
    • the wages and working conditions of the employment are sufficient to attract and retain Canadian citizens and permanent residents

Note that the provincial endorsement certificate is a separate document to the WPSL. Work permit applicants do not need to submit the endorsement certificate with their application. 

Application assessment

When officers are reviewing the work permit application in the AIP category, the following factors should be reviewed.

Review in the offer of employment

When assessing if the work permit application meets the requirements of the AIP, officers should review the offer of employment that appears under the “Employment Details” tab in the Global Case Management System (GCMS) for information provided by the employer or the matching fields on the IMM 5802 form (if employer authorized to use it):

Field Considerations
LMIA exemption code

C18

This is the code that the employer selected in the offer submitted in the Employer Portal.

Ensure that the administrative code matches the description in “Requirements exemptions met”.
Requirements exemptions met

This field must indicate that there is an approved endorsement from an Atlantic province.

Information in this field outlines how the job position or the foreign national meets the IMP requirements. This should not be cut and pasted from IRCC’s website.
NOC and job title This should be consistent with the WPSL issued by the endorsing Atlantic province
Duties and job requirements

This field should show that the duties align with the lead statement and main duties, as stated in the NOC code.

These are the activities that the foreign national will be performing.

See the note under Qualifying work experience.
Provincial or federal certification, licensing or registration Documentary evidence should be provided with the application; however, some occupations may require the foreign national to write an exam after they enter Canada (for example, for a licence from a regulated body or a first aid certificate).

Qualifying language results

Applicants under the AIP must demonstrate a minimum language requirement in each of the 4 language skill areas of the Canadian Language Benchmarks (CLB) for English or the Niveaux de compétence linguistique canadiens (NCLC) for French, based on an IRCC-approved language test.

Officers must be satisfied that the applicant has demonstrated a minimum language level of

  • 5 for a NOC Training, Education, Experience and Responsibilities (TEER) 0, 1, 2 or 3 job offer
  • 4 for a NOC TEER 4 job offer

Note: The test results must be less than 2 years old on the date the work permit application is received, unless the candidate has already submitted an APR under the AIP with valid language test results that were less than 2 years old at the time the APR was submitted.

Qualifying educational credential

Skilled workers

Applicants other than those applying as international graduates must demonstrate at the date of application that they have 1 of the following educational credentials.

Officers must ensure that the applicant has demonstrated a minimum of either

  • a Canadian 1-year post-secondary credential, or the foreign equivalent with an educational credential assessment (ECA), where the job offer is TEER category 0 or 1, or
  • a Canadian high school diploma, or the foreign equivalent with an ECA, where the job offer is TEER 2, 3 or 4

Note: Foreign credentials where the program of study was completed outside of Canada must be supported by an ECA report from an agency approved by IRCC. The assessment must be less than 5 years old, unless the candidate has already submitted an APR under the AIP with a valid ECA at the time the APR was submitted. See also Educational Credential Assessment requirements.

ECAs for eligible pharmacists with an endorsement from Nova Scotia

The Nova Scotia Pharmacy Regulator (NSPR) offers a streamlined licensing pathway for certain pharmacists licensed abroad.

Nova Scotia AIP applicants who are eligible for NSPR’s streamlined licensing pathway and hold a job offer as a pharmacist endorsed by Nova Scotia may obtain their ECA from

  1. any general purpose IRCC-designated ECA organization, or
  2. the Pharmacy Examining Board of Canada (PEBC)

Applicants who are not eligible under this streamlined pathway must obtain their ECA from the PEBC.

Important: Equivalency assessments will include an assessment of the authenticity of the applicant’s completed foreign educational credentials by the designated organization. The assessment provided by the designated organization is not conclusive evidence of the authenticity of the foreign educational credentials.

If an officer has concerns about the authenticity of an applicant’s foreign educational credentials, they must follow the procedural fairness guidelines.

International graduates

Recent international graduates applying under the AIP must meet the recent graduate requirements in order for their educational credential to be eligible:

The credential must be 1 of the following:

  • a post-secondary degree
  • a post-secondary diploma
  • a post-secondary certificate
  • a trade or apprenticeship credential

It must also have been obtained

  • as a full-time student (as defined by the designated learning institution)
  • while the foreign national had valid temporary resident status for the entire period of study they were working toward the credential as well as authorization for any work or study completed during that period
  • on the completion of a program of study that was at least 2 years in duration (or at least 4 academic terms)
  • from a recognized post-secondary institution in 1 of the 4 Atlantic provinces (New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, or Newfoundland and Labrador)
  • within the 2 years before the date on which either the APR or the work permit application is made (if the APR has not yet been submitted)

An educational credential is not eligible if the credential was obtained in a study or training program where

  • the study of English or French as a second language was at least half of the program
  • distance learning was at least half of the program
  • a scholarship or fellowship stipulated that the recipient return to their home country to apply the knowledge and skills gained

Qualifying work experience

AIP applicants that do not meet the recent graduate requirements must meet the qualifying work experience requirements.

A foreign national meets the work experience requirements if

  • they have at least 1 year of full-time paid work experience (or the equivalent in part-time work experience, meaning at least 1,560 hours) within the 5 years preceding the date of the work permit application
  • the work is in occupations that are listed in TEER 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4, exclusive of restricted occupations
  • they performed the actions described in the lead statement for the occupation as stated in the NOC 
  • they have performed a substantial number of the main duties of the occupation as per the NOC description, including all of the essential duties
  • the experience was acquired while the foreign national was authorized to work and had temporary resident status

Note: Any period of self-employment is not included in calculating a period of work experience.

TEER category in the offer of employment

Under the regulations applicable to the Atlantic Immigration Class [R87.3(6)(d)], the occupation in the offer of employment may be for a higher TEER (or skill level) than the qualifying work experience. When assessing if the applicant can perform the work sought as per paragraph R200(3)(a), officers should consider all of the following:

  • that the employer is satisfied the applicant can perform the higher TEER occupation
  • that the employer has worked with a settlement service provider organization to develop a settlement plan for the employee and their family
  • that the province has assessed the offer and determined that it is genuine as per the agreement
  • that during their work experience at the lower TEER occupation, the applicant may have performed some of the duties of the higher TEER occupation

Important: The offer of employment must be for a higher or equal TEER occupation than the qualifying work experience.

Note: The only exception when an offer of employment can be for a lower TEER occupation than the qualifying work experience is where the experience was acquired while working as either

  • a registered nurse (TEER 1)
    • NOC 31301 
      or
  • a licensed practical nurse (TEER 2)

    • NOC 32101

      and they are being offered employment as

      • a nurse’s aide, orderly or patient services associate (TEER 3)
        • NOC 33102

        or

      • as a home support worker (TEER 4)
        • NOC 44101

Scenarios/Examples

The following examples demonstrate potential scenarios and considerations and are not meant to be exhaustive. Officers should consult their supervisor or manager if they are unsure how to proceed.

Scenario 1

An applicant has worked as a fish and seafood plant worker under NOC 94142, a TEER 4 occupation, and the offer of employment under the AIP is for a fish processing supervisor under NOC 92012, a TEER 2 occupation:

  • The worker may have sufficient years of experience, in which case they could progress easily to the higher occupation and may satisfy an officer that they can perform the work sought.
  • They may have acted in that occupation, in which case they may have sufficient experience to satisfy an officer that they can perform the work sought.
  • There may not be evidence that they have sufficient experience or skills, in which case the officer may not be satisfied that they can perform the work sought.

Scenario 2

An applicant has worked as a fish processing supervisor under NOC 92012, a TEER 2 occupation, and the offer of employment under the AIP is for a fish and seafood plant worker under NOC 94142, a TEER 4 occupation:

  • The application cannot be approved because the occupation in the offer is for a lower TEER than the qualifying work experience.

Scenario 3

An applicant has worked as a registered nurse under NOC 31301, a TEER  1 occupation, and the offer of employment under the AIP is for a nurse’s aide under NOC 33102, a TEER 3 occupation:

  • The officer may be satisfied that the worker can perform the work sought because even though the work experience was gained at a higher TEER occupation, these occupations are some of the few for which there is an exception.

Refusal

If an officer is not satisfied that all the requirements of section R200, including the assessment under paragraph R204(c) are met, they must record their reasons and outline the rationale underlying the decision, as well as the facts and elements considered. They must also provide an explanation for the decision in a case note.

Refusal reasons should clearly indicate which criteria or what requirement of section R200 was not met and explain how the conclusion was reached. For assistance, officers can follow the steps in Decision-making: Standard of review and process for making a reasonable decision.

Open work permits for family members

Officers may issue an open work permit to family members of an AIP or AIPP applicant if the applicant has a job offer in any TEER category (for example, a 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4 position).

  • For family members of foreign nationals authorized to work in a high-skilled occupation (TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3), see Family members of foreign nationals authorized to work in high-skilled occupations (TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3) – [R205(c)(ii) – C41 and C46].
  • For family members of foreign nationals authorized to work in a low-skilled occupation (TEER 4), see Family members of foreign nationals authorized to work in low-skilled occupations (TEER 4 or 5) – [R205(c)(ii) – C47 and C48].
