Facilitated access to temporary residence for applicants on the pathway to permanent residence: Family reunification for Ukrainian nationals

This section contains policy, procedures and guidance used by IRCC staff. It is posted on the department’s website as a courtesy to stakeholders.

These instructions apply to IRCC employees. Given that applicants cannot submit their applications at a port of entry, the instructions do not apply to border services officers.

The instructions on this page should be reviewed in conjunction with the following:

  • Open work permit – General processing and issuance
  • Conditions and validity period on work permits
  • International students
  • Visitor Records – In-Canada applications
  • Restoration of status
  • Eligibility for extending temporary resident status
  • Extensions for minors

The Temporary public policy to facilitate access to temporary residence for certain Ukrainians and their family members who have applied under the Temporary public policy to grant permanent residence to certain Ukrainian nationals with family in Canada gives certain Ukrainian nationals currently in Canada and their eligible family members who have applied under the Pathway to permanent residence: Family reunification for Ukrainian nationals options to maintain or restore temporary resident (TR) status in Canada while the processing of their permanent residence (PR) application continues. This new public policy came into effect on January 16, 2026, and will expire on March 31, 2027.

This temporary public policy allows officers to exempt eligible applicants from certain requirements of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (IRPA) and the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations (IRPR), facilitating the following applications for eligible applicants, subject to standard fees:

  • new open work permits under R200,
  • work permit renewals under R201(1),
  • study permits under R216, and
  • restoration or extension of their stay in Canada as a temporary resident, as applicable.

There is also a measure, valid until March 31, 2026, to facilitate applications for restoration outside of the usual 90-day restoration period.

Eligibility

Principal foreign national

To be eligible under this facilitative measure, the principal foreign national must:

  • be a national of Ukraine, or be or have been the spouse or common-law partner of a national of Ukraine;
  • have submitted an application under the Temporary public policy to grant permanent residence to certain Ukrainian nationals with family in Canada that has been accepted into processing (meaning it has been promoted in GCMS)
    • the permanent residence application must not have been already refused or withdrawn
  • be applying for
    • an open work permit under R200,
    • a work permit renewal under R201(1),
    • a study permit under R216, or
    • restoration of their temporary resident status
      • If they have been out of status for over 90 days, they must apply on or before March 31, 2026
  • be in Canada, and
    • have valid temporary resident status

      • they must be in Canada when the application is received and also when their document is issued,

        or

    • if after March 31, 2026, submitted an application to restore their temporary resident status under subsection 182(1) of the Regulations within 90 days of having lost that status

      • they must remain in Canada while the application for restoration is processed

        or

    • applied in Canada on or before March 31, 2026, to restore their temporary resident status even if the application was submitted outside of the usual 90-day restoration period. For an application to be eligible
      • the application for restoration must not have been refused,
      • they must have had valid temporary resident status at the time the PR application was made, or they must have lost temporary resident status within the past 90 days before making the PR application and
      • they must remain in Canada while the application for restoration is processed.

Family members in Canada

To be eligible under this facilitative measure, family members must:

  • meet the definition of family member provided in subsection R1(3) in respect of the principal foreign national who has applied for and been found to be eligible under this public policy
  • have been included as an accompanying family member in the principal foreign national’s permanent residence application
  • be applying for
    • an open work permit under R200,
    • a work permit renewal under R201(1),
    • a study permit under R216, or
    • restoration of their temporary resident status
      • If they have been out of status for over 90 days, they must apply on or before March 31, 2026
  • be in Canada, and
    • have valid temporary resident status

      • they must be in Canada when the application is received and also when their document is issued,

        or

    • if after March 31,2026, submitted an application to restore their temporary resident status under subsection 182(1) of the Regulations within 90 days of having lost that status

      • they must remain in Canada while the application for restoration is processed

        or

    • applied in Canada on or before March 31, 2026, to restore their temporary resident status even if the application was submitted outside of the usual 90 day restoration period; For an application to be eligible:
      • the application for restoration must not have been refused,
      • they must have had valid temporary resident status at the time the PR application was made, or they must have lost temporary resident status within the past 90 days before making that application and
      • they must remain in Canada while the application for restoration is processed.

Physical presence and status in Canada

Applicants who are in Canada without valid temporary resident status may apply to restore their status. If they are beyond the standard 90-day restoration period, they must apply on or before March 31, 2026, to restore their status.

Important: To be eligible for facilitation under this temporary public policy, the applicant must be physically in Canada and must have valid temporary resident status, or have applied for restoration within 90 days of having lost that status, or, if past the 90-day restoration window, applied for restoration under these measures on or before March 31, 2026. An individual who has applied for facilitation under the public policy and who does not require restoration may leave Canada while their application is pending, but must be in Canada with valid temporary resident status or be eligible for restoration of status at the time the decision is rendered to benefit from facilitation under the public policy. Please note that, a foreign national who leaves Canada no longer holds valid temporary resident status and must report to the port of entry where a decision will be made whether to permit re-entry as a temporary resident. Applicants that require restoration of status must remain in Canada until a decision is made.

Officers should confirm that the applicant is physically in Canada before granting exemptions under these measures and issuing a document. In addition to considering all documents provided by the applicant, officers may consult the Entry/Exit Program.

Regulatory exemptions

Under this temporary public policy, the following exemptions apply:

  • Paragraphs R199(a) to (i): the requirements that a foreign national must meet in order to apply for a work permit after entering Canada;
  • Paragraph R200(1)(c): the requirement to be described under subparagraphs 200(1)(c)(i), (ii), (ii.1) or (iii) of the Regulations;
  • Section R213: the requirement to apply for a study permit before entering Canada;
  • Paragraphs R215(1)(a) to (g): the requirements that a foreign national must meet in order to apply for a study permit after entering Canada.

In addition, if they applied in Canada on or before March 31, 2026, for restoration of their temporary resident status under subsection 182(1) of the Regulations, which has not been refused, the following exemptions also apply:

  • Paragraph 41(a) of the Act: the requirement not to be inadmissible for failing to comply with the Act, as long as the non-compliance only relates to overstaying the period authorized for their stay, or working or studying without authorization;
  • Subsection R182(1): the requirement for a visitor, worker or student to apply for restoration within 90 days after losing temporary resident status;
  • Paragraph R200(3)(e): the requirement that a work permit not be issued if they have engaged in unauthorized work or study in Canada or have failed to comply with the conditions of a previous permit or authorization.

Application submission

Open work permit

Principal foreign nationals and their eligible family members may apply for or renew an open work permit (administrative code R01) under these measures. Applicants who apply to renew their work authorization before it expires will maintain their existing status and authorization while their application is being processed.

Applicants may apply to restore their status and apply for a work permit using the same application, or they may apply for a work permit renewal without requesting restoration if they have valid temporary resident status. If they have fallen out of status and they are beyond the standard 90-day restoration period, they will have until March 31, 2026 to apply to restore their status as a temporary resident. If they are within the 90-day restoration period, they are eligible to apply for restoration in accordance with the provisions of the Regulations (facilitation for restoration under the measures is not required). Applications must be submitted online unless an exception to applying online applies (see Method of application for temporary resident visas (TRVs), electronic travel authorizations (eTAs), study permits, work permits and visitor records).

TRP holders who have a work permit and are seeking to renew their work permit are required to apply for another TRP by submitting a paper application. TRP holders do not maintain their temporary resident status during the processing period as they do not meet the requirements of subsection R183(5), and those who let their status lapse are not eligible for restoration.

As the public policy exempts eligible applicants from paragraphs R199(a) to (i) and R200(1)(c), applicants who are eligible may apply for an initial work permit as a visitor from within Canada. If they require restoration, they must complete an IMM 5708 application to remain in Canada as a visitor and apply for restoration. They must also complete the application for a work permit (IMM 5710). The application to restore temporary resident status and remain in Canada as a visitor will be assessed before the application for a work permit.

Study permit

Principal foreign nationals and their eligible family members may apply for a study permit under these measures. Applicants who apply to renew their study authorization before it expires will maintain their existing status and authorization while their application is being processed.

Applicants may apply for restoration of status and a study permit in the same application, or they may apply for a study permit without restoration if they have valid temporary resident status. If they have fallen out of status and they are beyond the standard 90-day restoration period, they will have until March 31, 2026, to apply to restore their status as a temporary resident. Note that if an applicant is applying for a study permit renewal or for restoration of status within 90 days, they may do so under the regular study permit stream (facilitation under the measures is not required).

Applications must be submitted online unless an exception to applying online applies (see Method of application for temporary resident visas (TRVs), electronic travel authorizations (eTAs), study permits, work permits and visitor records).

TRP holders who have a study permit and are seeking to apply for another study permit are required to apply for another TRP by submitting a paper application. TRP holders do not maintain their temporary resident status during the processing period as they do not meet the requirements of subsection R183(5), and those who let their status lapse are not eligible for restoration.

As the public policy exempts eligible applicants from R213 and paragraphs 215(1)(a) to (g), applicants who are eligible may apply for a study permit as a visitor from within Canada. If they require restoration, they must complete an IMM 5708 application to remain in Canada as a visitor and apply for restoration. They must also complete the application for a study permit (IMM 5709). The application to restore temporary resident status and remain in Canada as a visitor will be assessed before the application for a study permit.

Extension of temporary resident status (Visitor record)

Principal foreign nationals and their eligible family members in the visitor class who have fallen out of status and are beyond the standard 90-day restoration period, will have until March 31, 2026, to apply to restore their status as a temporary resident in the visitor class. If they are still in status or within the 90-day restoration period, they may apply to extend their stay or restore their status as per existing regulations [paragraphs R181(1) and R182(1)] (facilitation under the measures is not required). For further information, see Restoration of temporary resident status.

Applicants applying to extend their temporary resident status must meet the criteria: Eligibility for extending temporary resident status. Applicants who apply for an extension of their temporary resident status before it expires will maintaintheir existing status and authorization while their application is being processed.

Those applying for restoration of temporary resident status should be in Canada when they submit their application and remain in Canada until a final decision is made.

Documentary evidence

The following documentary evidence must be provided with the application under these measures:

  • Payment receipt
  • Specific documents for the principal foreign national or accompanying family members, as stated below, and
  • Any applicable application forms and/or documents required to demonstrate eligibility for the status and authorization they are applying for (work, study, visitor).

Principal foreign national

The principal foreign national must provide:

  • Proof they are a national of Ukraine, or the spouse or common-law partner of a national of Ukraine; either
    • A copy of a Ukraine passport
      • Ukrainians who face challenges obtaining a passport may be facilitated on a case-by-case basis. See Ukrainian passport delays.

      or

    • A copy of their passport and
      • Proof they are (or were) the spouse or common-law partner of a national of Ukraine. This could include:
        • a marriage certificate, or
        • Statutory Declaration of Common-Law Union [IMM 5409], or

        • other evidence to satisfy the officer that the relationship is/was genuine

          and

        • a copy of the national of Ukraine’s passport, or
        • other evidence to satisfy the officer that the relationship is/was with a national of Ukraine
  • Proof that they are physically present in Canada, which could include:
    • Photocopies of passport pages clearly showing the stamp made by Canadian authorities on the applicant’s most recent entry to Canada
    • Copies of boarding passes for inbound flight to Canada
    • Copies of a current lease or rental agreement
    • A letter from a landlord or property owner attesting to residence and specifying dates
    • Most recent utility bills or other bills that contain the applicant’s residential address
    • Copy of a bank statement or credit card statement with the applicant’s address showing day-to-day transactions
    • Employment documents or personal services contract
    • Evidence of receipt of benefits from Canadian government programs
    • Other proof that satisfies the officer
    • Officers may also review, but should not rely solely on, entry/exit data (see Entry/Exit Program).
  • An explanation that they are applying under the Temporary public policy to facilitate access to temporary residence for certain Ukrainians and their family members who have applied under the Temporary public policy to grant permanent residence to certain Ukrainian nationals with family in Canada
    • If they are applying for restoration and concurrently applying for a new authorization (visitor to worker or student, student to worker, etc.) they should indicate this in the letter so that officers are aware and can make sure that the restoration application is assessed before the application for the new authorization).
  • Optional - Family Information form [IMM 5707]
    • If the officer finds that they require the IMM 5707 for their assessment, it should be requested. Applications should not be refused due to a missing IMM 5707.

The officer must also be satisfied of the following:

  • That the PR application has been submitted and accepted into processing. Examples of proof include:
    • If the applicant has received an acknowledgement of receipt (AOR) from their PR application, they can provide the AOR.

      Note: Applicants are not required to submit their AOR as proof. If the AOR is not provided by the applicant, officers must confirm that the AOR was sent in the Global Case Management System (GCMS), and if not, hold processing of the application until the officer can confirm that the application has been accepted into processing. Refer to Application assessment for more instructions on this requirement.

  • The applicant has valid temporary resident status or has applied for restoration:
    • The applicant may include a copy of their existing work or study permit or proof of their valid status as a visitor (through passport stamps or a Visitor Record), or
    • the applicant may apply for restoration as part of this application, if eligible, and should include a copy of their expired work or study permit or proof of the date they lost status (through passport stamps or a Visitor Record).

Family members in Canada

Accompanying family members must provide:

  • Copy of their passport
  • Proof of relationship
    • For example, a marriage certificate, Statutory Declaration of Common-Law Union [IMM 5409], birth certificate of a child that shows parent name, etc.
  • If the family members are applying individually after the principal foreign national receives approval from IRCC on their TR application(s) to be authorized to work, study or to stay in Canada as a visitor, the family members are instructed to include a letter in their application indicating:
    • the principal foreign national’s work permit number, study permit number or the visitor record number issued by IRCC and/or the principal foreign national’s PR application number, and
    • that they are applying under this public policy.

Note: If the family members are applying with the principal foreign national as a family group, they are not required to include this letter in their application.

  • Proof that they are physically present in Canada (see the list of examples under the principal foreign national section).

The officer must also be satisfied of the following:

  • The applicant has valid temporary resident status or has applied for restoration:
    • The applicant may include a copy of their existing work or study permit or proof of their valid status as a visitor (through passport stamps or a Visitor Record), or
    • the applicant may apply for restoration as part of this application, if eligible, and should include a copy of their expired work or study permit or proof of the date they lost status (through passport stamps or a Visitor Record).
  • Any family members applying under these measures are included in the application for PR.

Open work permit

In addition to the specific documents required to apply to this public policy, applicants applying for a work permit must also submit the usual documents required for a work permit application, as listed in their personalized document checklist (Application to Change Conditions, Extend my Stay or Remain in Canada as a Worker (IMM 5710), and other forms if applicable).

Applicants are instructed that they must also indicate in the online application tool that they are applying for a work permit under an active public policy announced by IRCC for them to trigger the open work permit results. In the form, they will enter “Ukraine OWP” in the job title box and “Ukraine OWP – APR applicant in Canada public policy” in the brief description of duties box. This will assist officers in identifying applications under these measures, to which the appropriate ORG ID and Special Program Code should be added. Officers can also identify these applications by reviewing the documents provided under ‘Client Information’, as applicants are instructed to provide an explanation that they are applying under these measures.

Study permit

Applicants must submit their study permit applications in Canada by following the instructions to extend their study permit.

Study permit applicants must meet the requirements, which include submitting the letter of acceptance and provincial or territorial attestation letter, as applicable.

Officers should refer to Temporary Residents: Students for guidance.

Officers can identify these applications by reviewing the documents provided under ‘Client Information’, as applicants are instructed to provide an explanation that they are applying under these measures.

Extension of temporary resident status (Visitor record)

Applicants applying to extend or restore their status as a temporary resident must submit a complete application. See Temporary resident: Application intake assessment.

Officers can identify these applications by reviewing the documents provided under ‘Client Information’, as applicants are instructed to provide an explanation that they are applying under these measures.

Application assessment

When officers are reviewing the application under these temporary measures, the following factors should be reviewed:

  • The supporting documentary evidence to confirm that the eligibility criteria— including nationality, family relationship, TR status and physical presence in Canada—under the measures are met.
    • If the officer is not satisfied that all of the criteria are met, they may request additional supporting documents.
    • If the officer is still not satisfied, the TR application(s) should be refused for not meeting the requirement of the temporary public policy to be granted the relevant exemptions and not otherwise having demonstrated that they meet the requirements for the authorization being applied for or for restoration of status, as the case may be.
  • Confirmation that the Acknowledgment of Receipt (AOR) has been issued in the associated PR application:
    • If the PR application has been placed into processing, the officer may continue processing the TR application(s).
    • If the PR application has not yet been placed into processing, the officer should hold the TR application(s) for 30 days to ensure enough time has been provided for the application to be accepted into processing.
    • If the PR application was not accepted into processing, the TR application(s) should be refused for not meeting the requirement of the temporary public policy to be granted the relevant exemptions and not otherwise having demonstrated that they meet the requirements for the authorization being applied for or for restoration of status, as the case may be.
    • If no PR application was submitted, the TR application should be refused.
  • If the applicant is out of status and applying for restoration on or before March 31, 2026 outside of the usual 90-day restoration period, the officer must
    • compare the date the applicant lost TR status to the time and date the PR application was made, and
    • ensure that the applicant had valid temporary resident status at the time the PR application was made, or that they had not lost temporary resident status more than 90 days before making that application.

      Note that “the time the application was made” differs from the date the application was accepted into processing (when the AOR was sent). The officer can check the received date “Rec'd Date” in the PR application in GCMS to confirm when the application was made.

      Note that if the applicant lost their status more than 90 days ago, the online application directs the applicant to enter that their status expired on January 16, 2026.This is a workaround to allow them to submit their application under the public policy. Officers must review the application to confirm the date that their TR status was lost (applicants are instructed to provide documents to confirm their TR status).

Officers should review all of the information provided under the ‘Client Information’ field, to ensure no details are missed (for example, if the applicant is applying for restoration as well as an initial application for a work or study permit, the officer needs to assess the application for restoration first).

Genuine relationship

As per section R4, a foreign national shall not be considered a spouse or a common-law partner of a person if the marriage or common-law partnership

  • was entered into primarily for the purpose of acquiring any status or privilege under the IRPA, or
  • is not genuine.

If officers have concerns as to the genuineness of the relationship between the principal foreign national and their spouse or common-law partner, they may request further documentation/information or conduct an interview to confirm that the relationship between the dependent spouse or common-law partner and the principal foreign national is genuine and is not a relationship of convenience.

Minor children

Minor children can apply for a study permit under existing study permit streams or may study on a visitor record if they are eligible (see Study permits: Guidelines on minor children).

Dependent children and working age

There is no set minimum age to be eligible for a work permit. The submission of work permit applications on behalf of minor children is at the discretion of their parent(s) or authorized guardian(s).

The “working age” varies across provincial and territorial jurisdictions and there is no minimum age specified in the IRPR or the IRPA for issuing a work permit.

Therefore, unless there are reasonable grounds for an officer to be satisfied that a dependent child is unable to perform the work sought or any other reasons for refusal, minors may be issued a work permit.

Please refer to the instructions on Who qualifies as a dependent child to confirm if an applicant meets the definition of a dependent child in relation to the principal foreign national.

Admissibility

If the principal foreign national and their accompanying family members applied for restoration of status on or before March 31, 2026, they may be exempted from the following provision:

  • non-compliance (paragraph A41(a)) for failing to comply with the IRPA, related to overstaying their temporary resident status and working or studying without authorization

Applicants under this public policy are subject to all other applicable admissibility requirements not exempted under this or another public policy.

Subsequent renewals

Principal foreign nationals and their eligible family members who have a PR application in process that has not been withdrawn or refused, and who were issued or granted one of the following:

  • a work permit under section R200
  • a work permit renewal under subsection R201(1)
  • a study permit under section R216
  • restoration of temporary resident status, under subsection R182(1)

are eligible to renew their authorization, or apply to work or study as applicable under these measures.

Fees and refunds

Officers should refer to the Fee list.

Open work permit fees

All foreign nationals applying for an open work permit under this public policy are exempt only from the open work permit holder fee of $100, as applicants are not applying under paragraph R200(1)(c) and are therefore not required to pay the fee under subsection R303.2(1). All other applicable fees, including the work permit processing fee, must be paid.

Officers should be aware that both the work permit fee and the open work permit fee are automatically triggered in the online application tool.

To reduce the volume of refunds, applicants are instructed to pay the work permit processing fee outside the online tool and upload their receipt under Proof of Fee Exemption. For more information on applicant instructions, consult Permanent residence for Ukrainian nationals with family members in Canada Apply for an open work permit, study permit or to extend your stay in Canada as a temporary resident.

Study permit and TR extension fees

All applicable fees must be paid.

Refunds

For applicants who have paid the open work permit holder fee, officers must initiate a refund of the open work permit holder fee only, as per normal office procedures.

Important: If this fee is paid in error, it must be refunded as per the processing office’s normal refund procedures.

None of the other fees should be refunded.

Restoration

Although there is an exemption from the requirement for a visitor, worker or student to apply for restoration within 90 days after losing temporary resident status if they applied in Canada for restoration of their temporary resident status on or before March 31, 2026, there is no exemption from the fee.

Important: Applicants applying for restoration are required to pay the restoration fee.

Ukrainian passport delays

The department is aware of the difficulty and delays Ukrainian nationals are experiencing in regard to renewing their passports.

All temporary residents in Canada must apply to extend their status, ideally before the expiry date of their current visitor record, work or study permit. If temporary residents apply before the expiry date, they can continue working or studying under the prior authorization and will have maintained status until IRCC makes a decision on their application. Note that TRP holders and applicants do not benefit from maintained status.

Officers may request a written explanation from the applicant if it is not possible for the Ukrainian national to have their passport extended before the expiry date of their current passport and their visitor record, work or study permit. The Ukrainian national may be asked to provide a copy of their expired passport, along with a letter explaining what steps they have taken to get their passport renewed, and to submit their new passport as soon as they receive it.

A25.1 cannot be used to exempt an applicant from the requirements of the public policy; however, if there is an applicant, who meets the requirements of the public policy, but does not meet the requirements of the regulations related to visitor extensions, work or study permits, separate from the public policy, the officer may consider granting a waiver under A25.1 from the requirements of the regulations related to visitor extensions, work or study permits, separate from the public policy.

For example, if an applicant seeking exemptions under this public policy does not meet other requirements of the Act or Regulations, such as the requirement to have a valid travel document, officers may take into consideration the circumstances of the applicant, and may use their delegated authority under A25.1 to consider a waiver of those requirements.

In cases where the officer uses their discretion to consider A25.1 and subsequently decides there are insufficient humanitarian and compassionate considerations to justify the exemption, the applicant is owed procedural fairness in order to respond to the potential refusal.

Conditions (mandatory when no medical exam is completed)

Officers should ensure that instructions provided in Medical conditions to be imposed for an unrestricted open work permit are followed.

Applicants with valid IME results do not require a condition on their OWP.

If a medical examination was not completed, one of the conditions below must be imposed. The specific occupational sector restriction will depend on whether the person has resided in a designated or non-designated country.

  • For persons who have not resided in a designated country:

    “Not authorized to work in 1) child care, 2) primary or secondary school teaching, 3) health services field occupations.”

GCMS remarks

Processing fees

$155 work permit processing fee
$85 biometric fee, if applicable.

Restoration fee, if applying for restoration.

All foreign nationals applying for an open work permit under this public policy are exempt from the open work permit holder fee of $100 as the fee is not referenced in subsection R303.2(1). All other applicable fees must be paid.
Biometrics

Work permit applicants are required to provide biometric information and pay the biometric fee. The regular biometric exemptions apply (for example, age or 1 in 10).

Study permit

Study permit validity must be tied to the specified period of study, or passport validity.

Officers should refer to Temporary Residents: Students for additional guidance.

Extension of temporary resident status (Visitor record)

Officers should consider extending/restoring visitor status for 3 years, or to match the principal foreign national’s validity, or until passport expiry, whichever comes first.

Refer to Visitor Records – In-Canada applications for processing instructions.

Refusal

If an officer is not satisfied that all the requirements of R200, R201(1), or other applicable sections are met, they must record their reasons and outline the rationale underlying the decision as well as the facts and elements considered. They must also provide an explanation for the decision in a case note.

Refusal reasons should clearly indicate which criteria or what requirements were not met and explain how the conclusion was reached. For assistance, officers can follow the steps in Decision making: Standard of review and process for making a reasonable decision.
