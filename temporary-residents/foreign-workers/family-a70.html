In-Canada family members – [R207(e) – A70] – International Mobility Program

This section contains policy, procedures and guidance used by IRCC staff. It is posted on the department’s website as a courtesy to stakeholders.

These instructions apply to IRCC employees. Given that applicants in Canada cannot submit their applications at a port of entry, the instructions do not apply to border services officers.

The instructions on this page should be reviewed in conjunction with the following:

  • Open work permit – General processing and issuance
  • Conditions and validity period on work permits

Section 207 of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations (IRPR) applies to applicants who have been determined eligible as members of certain in-Canada permanent residence classes and includes protected persons and family members, whether or not they have applied for permanent resident status.

An unrestricted open work permit may be issued to a foreign national who has met the requirements of section R207 under their respective class.

As per section R202, applicants who are issued a work permit under paragraph R207(c) or (d) do not, by reason only of being issued a work permit, become temporary residents.

Eligibility

To be issued an open work permit under paragraph R207(e), the applicant must meet all of the criteria of their specific member class at the time of decision on the work permit application.

The applicant must

  • have submitted the application for an open work permit using the online IRCC Secure Account (referred to as MyAccount or the Authorized Paid Representative Portal) as per section R9.2
    • Only applicants who meet the requirements of section R9.4 and section R9.5 may use the paper application form.
  • be the family member of the principal applicant as identified in subsection R1(3), which includes
    • the principal applicant’s dependent child
    • their spouse/partner’s dependent child
    • a dependent child of a dependent child referred to in paragraph (b)
  • be the family member of the principal applicant as described in any of paragraphs R207(b) to (d)
  • be in Canada
    • However, applicants are not required to live at the same residential address as the family member who met the criteria of the specific member class.

Documentary evidence

  • confirmation that the principal applicant meets the eligibility requirements of the member class specified in paragraphs R207(b) to (d)
  • proof of relationship (for example, a birth certificate)

Assessing the application

Processing officers will ensure that the general processing instructions for the open work permit are met.

To be issued an open work permit under paragraph R207(e), the applicant must meet the definition of a family member at subsection R1(3) in relation to an individual described at paragraphs R207(b) to (d).

The definition of family member does not require that the individual in question be an accompanying family member or be included on a PR application to qualify for an open work permit pursuant to paragraph R207(e).

The principal applicant (PA) is not required to hold a work permit issued pursuant to section R207 in order for the family member to qualify for the open work permit under paragraph R207(e). What must be confirmed is that the PA meets the criteria of their specific class.

For example, the PA may be a protected person within the meaning of subsection 95(2) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (IRPA), but not hold an open work permit issued pursuant to paragraph R207(c). The dependent becomes eligible for the open work permit under paragraph R207(e) based on the fact that the PA met the criteria of the class, without the PA needing to hold an open work permit pursuant to paragraph R207(c).

In order for a foreign national to be issued a work permit pursuant to paragraph R207(e), they must be the family member of a person described in any of paragraphs R207(b) to (d), they must be in Canada, and they must meet the requirements of section R200.

They are not exempted from the requirement of paragraph R200(1)(b) and must be able to demonstrate that they will leave Canada at the end of their authorized period of stay. This depends on an individualized assessment by an officer, taking into account the facts of each case.

Dependent children and working age

There is no set minimum age to be eligible for a work permit. The submission of work permit applications on behalf of minor children is at the discretion of their parent(s) or authorized guardian(s).

The “working age” varies across provincial and territorial jurisdictions and there is no minimum age for work permit issuance specified in the IRPA or the IRPR.

Therefore, unless there are reasonable grounds for an officer to be satisfied that a dependent child is unable to perform the work sought or any other reasons for refusal, minors may be issued a work permit.

Please refer to the instructions on Who qualifies as a dependent child to confirm if an applicant meets the definition of a dependent child in relation to the principal applicant.

Refusal

When officers are not satisfied that the specific eligibility factors for this administrative code are met, they need to clearly document their reasons in the refusal note.

A decision is reasonable when the reviewing court is able to trace the decision maker’s rationale without encountering fatal flaws in the overarching logic and is satisfied that there is a line of analysis within the given reasons that could lead the tribunal from the evidence before it to the decision maker’s conclusion. In other words, to be reasonable, a decision must be transparent, intelligible and justified.

The officer needs to engage with the documentary evidence that was provided by the applicant. Simply stating, “I have reviewed the submissions, and I am not satisfied that paragraph R207(e) is met”, is not sufficient for another reasonable person to understand the logic of the decision without reviewing all of the evidence again.

If an officer determines that the applicant does not meet the eligibility requirements, the work permit application should be refused.

Officers should follow the steps in Decision-making: Standard of review and process for making a reasonable decision when finalizing their refusal notes. The grounds in the refusal letter should reflect what the officer has stated in their notes.

Work permits shall be issued if all of the requirements stated in section R200 are met, including the requirement to be described in section R207. Therefore, refusal grounds should be linked to one of those requirements. If applicable, officers should explain why the applicant does not meet the eligibility requirements in section R207.

For example:

Applicant has not adequately demonstrated that they are a family member of a person described in any of paragraphs R207(b) to (d) for these reasons (explain).
