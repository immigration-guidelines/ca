​​​​​​​Responses to Information Requests (RIRs) are research reports on country conditions. They are requested by IRB decision-makers.

The database contains a seven-year archive of English and French RIRs. Earlier RIRs may be found on the European Country of Origin Information Network website.

RIR​s published by the IRB on its website may have attachments that are inaccessible due to technical constraints and may include translations of documents originally written in languages other than English or French. To obtain a copy of such attachments and/or translated version of the RIR attachments, please email us.​

Responses to Information Requests (RIRs) cite publicly accessible information available at the time of publication and within time constraints. A list of references and additional sources consulted are included in each RIR. Sources cited are considered the most current information available as of the date of the RIR.            

RIRs are not, and do not purport to be, conclusive as to the merit of any particular claim for refugee protection. Rather, they are intended to support the refugee determination process. More information on the methodology used by the Research Directorate can be found here.          

The assessment and weight to be given to the information in the RIRs are the responsibility of independent IRB members (decision-makers) after considering the evidence and arguments presented by the parties.           

The information presented in RIRs solely reflects the views and perspectives of the sources cited and does not necessarily reflect the position of the IRB or the Government of Canada.          

5 October 2026

GIN202693.E

Guinea: National identity card, including different versions, content, appearance, and security features; samples; requirements and procedures for obtaining them in the country and from abroad, including issuance timelines (2024–September 2026)

Research Directorate, Immigration and Refugee Board of Canada

This Response replaces Responses to Information Requests GIN105988 of October 2017 and GIN105004 of November 2014.

1. Overview of the National ID Card, Including Different Versions

Guinéenews, a news website, notes that the national ID card is [translation] "legal proof of nationality" and "makes it easier to access public, banking and administrative services" (2025-02-07). According to a 2020 article by Radio France internationale (RFI), as of January 2021 Guinea intended to implement a biometric identification card project with the aim of developing a digital civil registry with a unique number for each citizen (2020-10-07). The same source adds that the lack of a [translation] "reliable" civil registry had led to "numerous problems" (RFI 2020-10-07).

Ordinary Law of L/2023/0019/CNT on the Identification of Individuals in the Republic of Guinea (Loi ordinaire L/2023/0019/CNT portant identification des personnes physiques en République de Guinée) provides the following:

[translation]

Article 25: Individual and Mandatory Nature of the Secure Biometric National Identity Card

The Secure Biometric National Identity Card shall be issued on an individual basis and is mandatory for all Guinean citizens who have reached the age of sixteen.

… (Guinea 2023, bold and underline in original)

However, a report by staff of the World Bank, based on a survey conducted in 2024, notes that in Guinea 42% of women and 52% of men have IDs (Klapper, et al. 2025, xix, 70).

The 2020 RFI article notes that the biometric ID card will replace the [translation] "old, easily falsified … paper identity card" (2020-10-07). A February 2021 article on the news website Guinee114.com cites the director of the national ID card project for Guinée's Ministry of Security and Civil Protection (ministère de la Sécurité et de la Protection civile) as indicating that the police are no longer issuing the old ID card (2021-02-03).

Decree D/95/254/PRG/SGG of 1 September 1995 Introducing a New National Identity Card and a New Consular Card (Décret D/95/254/PRG/SGG du 1er septembre 1995, instituant une nouvelle carte nationale d’identité et une nouvelle carte consulaire), provides the following:

[translation]

ARTICLE 2

…

The national identity card is valid for a renewable term of five (5) years and is issued only to Guinean nationals.

ARTICLE 3

The national identity card is mandatory for all citizens from fifteen (15) years of age. …

ARTICLE 12

Every applicant for a national identity card registered in the central database receives a national identification number comprising fifteen digits that corresponds to the card number. (Guinea 1995)

However, a report by staff of the World Bank on global ID coverage, based on data collected in a 2017 survey with 994 respondents among the "civilian population over age 15" in Guinea, found that 44.6% of adults at or above the mandatory age have an ID, including 39.4% of women and 49.6% of men (Metz & Clark 2019-01-01, 3, 35).

1.1. Biometric National ID Card

Guinean media sources from February 2021 indicate that the delivery of the biometric ID card started in February 2021 (Guineematin.com 2021-02-03; Guinee360 2021-02-02). A September 2021 article by Guinéenews notes that while the biometric ID card was [translation] "officially launched" in September 2020, card production and issuance "actual[ly]" began in March 2021 (2021-09-28). Sources state that as of March 2023, public servants must have the biometric ID card to receive their salary (Guinéenews 2023-01-19; Ledjely.com 2023-02-10). The same media articles from 2023 describe obstacles to obtaining the card, including delays [see section 3.1.2 of this Response] (Guinéenews 2023-01-19; Ledjely.com 2023-02-10).

According to a joint statement by 4 government ministries published on the news website Guinee7.com, the state was to begin issuing the biometric national ID card free of charge on 14 April 2025 (2025-04-12).

Guineematin.com, a news website, notes that on 14 April 2025, residents in N'Zérékoré prefecture [in southeastern Guinea] wishing to obtain a national ID card were directed to the town hall to obtain a biometric birth certificate extract first, which is required to issue the biometric ID card (2025-04-14a). A December 2025 article by Africaguinee.com, a news website, notes that in Koundara prefecture [in the Boké region], there is a [translation] "shortage of materials necessary" for issuing biometric birth certificate extracts, which has "direct consequences" on obtaining the biometric ID card (2025-12-23). GuinéeSignal, a news website, indicates that in the prefecture of Lola [in the Nzérékoré region], the police station began distribution of the first batch of biometric ID cards on 25 October 2025 (2025-11-01). The same source adds that there is a [translation] "gradual national expansion" of the biometric ID card project (GuinéeSignal 2025-11-01). An article by the news website Lerevelateur224.com cites the Chief of Police in Kissidougou city [in southern Guinea] as indicating that the distribution of the biometric ID card began on 27 April 2026 in that city after the police received the first batch of printed ID cards for [translation] "over" 1,000 applicants (2026-04-28).

1.2. Paper ID Card

Sources report that the Guinea's government stopped issuing national ID cards in December 2014 (Guinéenews 2017-08-16; Jeune Afrique 2016-04-21; Guineematin.com 2016-04-06). According to Guinean media sources from 2016 and 2017, Guinea's government started them again in April 2016 (Guinéenews 2017-08-16; Jeune Afrique 2016-04-21). According to the news website Guineematin.com,

[translation]

[t]he Cabinet attaché for the DGPN [National Police Directorate (Direction générale de la police nationale)] said that "the migration process for the biometric identity cards is long and time-consuming, so the Ministry of Security and Civil Protection (ministère de la Sécurité et de la Protection Civile, MSPC), through the DGPN, has decided to continue with the ordinary cards." (2016-03-25)

Jeune Afrique explains that [translation] "identity cards are being issued again as a transitional solution until the government of Guinea is able to issue biometric identity cards, which are considered to be more secure" (2016-04-21). The 2021 Guinéenews article notes that the government had started a biometrics project in 2010, but it was [translation] "shelved" (2021-09-28).

2. Content, Appearance, and Security Features, Including Samples

2.1. Biometric ID Card

The 2023 law on the identification of persons in Guinea provides the following on the content and security features of the biometric ID card:

[translation]

Article 23: Certification and Issuance of the National Identity Card

The Secure Biometric National Identity Card certifies the holder's identity by indicating their Personal Identification Number.

…

Article 24: Data on the Secure Biometric National Identity Card

The Secure Biometric National Identity Card contains the following data:

• the Personal Identification Number;

• first names and last name;

• name authorized by law, if the person concerned so requests;

• day, month, year, and place of birth;

• gender;

• place of birth;

• nationality;

• occupation;

• place of residence;

• height;

• distinguishing features;

• photograph;

• fingerprints, except for persons with disabilities;

• the day, month, year, and place of issuance;

• the day, month, and year of expiration;

• the holder's electronic signature;

• the issuing authority;

• the issuing authority's electronic signature.

…

Article 63: Electronic Chip

The Secure Biometric National Identity Card and the Secure Biometric Passport are identity documents equipped with an electronic chip containing biometric data for authenticating the holder's information. (Guinea 2023, bold and underline in original)

The same law provides the following on the personal identification number (numéro personnel d'identification, NPI):

[translation]

Article 8: Digital Identification of Natural Persons

A personal identification number, abbreviated as NPI, is hereby established.

Upon registration in the national, commune, or consular registry of natural persons, a Personal Identification Number, derived from the national civil registry, shall be assigned to Guineans.

…

Article 9: Characteristics of the Personal Identification Number

The Personal Identification Number is:

• automatically assigned by the computer system of the national, commune, or consular registry of natural persons upon the initial registration of any natural person;

• determined in such a way as to prevent duplication in any form;

• permanent, unique, and valid for life; it cannot be modified or assigned to another person, either during the individual's lifetime or after their death;

• proof of the identity of the person to whom it has been assigned;

• recorded in the systems for the National Identity Card, passport, certificate of nationality, voter registration card, and driver's licence, as well as any other administrative document relating to the holder of the Personal Identification Number;

• assigned in the same manner to all applicants, without any discrimination or distinction.

It may be required in cases relating to the holder's estate or the certification of the parentage of his or her descendants.

The Personal Identification Number does not, in and of itself, constitute proof of nationality and cannot replace the identity or identification documents listed in Articles 21 and 37 of this Law.

A decree issued by the Council of Ministers shall establish the rules for the creation, management, and maintenance of the Personal Identification Number. (Guinea 2023, bold and underline in original)

Decree D/2025/051/PRG/CNRD/SGG of 14 April 2025, Establishing, Managing and Maintaining a Personal Identification Number (Décret D/2025/051/PRG/CNRD/SGG du 14 avril 2025, portant création, gestion et conservation d'un numéro personnel d'identification (NPI) en République de Guinée) provides the following:

[translation]

Article 2: A personal identification number consisting of eighteen (18) characters shall be instituted:

- for all Guineans upon issuance of the birth certificate;

…

Article 6: The NPI consists of eighteen (18) alphanumeric characters, including thirteen (13) digits, three (3) letters, and a two-digit (2) control key. This control key is calculated based on the first sixteen (16) characters. The NPI includes three (3) categories of values and one (1) verification key:

- biographical values related to an individual's personal history, such as:

    • gender: 1 for a man and 2 for a woman

    • year of birth: YY

    • month of birth: MM

- geographic values, such as:

    • prefecture of birth: PPP

    • commune of birth: CC

    • ward (quartier) of birth: QQ

    • the region code (RRR) and country code (LLL) for individuals born outside of Guinea

- random values: the random number

    • is a combination of four (4) random digits for individuals born in Guinea ! NNNN

    • and five (5) random digits for Guineans born abroad: NNNNN. (Guinea 2025a, bold in original)

Guinean media sources from 2021 cite the director of the national ID card project for the Ministry of Security and Civil Protection as indicating that the biometric ID card has a chip containing the holder's personal data and is created based on the individual's fingerprints and a unique identification number specific to each Guinean citizen (Guinee360 2021-02-03; Guinee114.com 2021-02-03).

A sample of a biometric identity card is available online in a lost and found announcement on the Facebook account of the Embassy of Guinee in Cairo (Guinea 2025-11-04).

2.2. Paper ID Card

Decree D/95/254/PRG/SGG of 1 September 1995 Introducing a New National Identity Card and a New Consular Card describes the physical characteristics of the national identity card as follows:

[translation]

  • Material: watermarked paper 90 gr/m2
  • Format: ID-2 or 70 x 100 mm
  • Printing: 4 colours offset printing with specific characteristics of the Republic of Guinea. It is entirely plasticized using a heat-seal laminating pouch that is 2 x 125 microns thick and 74 x 105 mm in format. In addition to its seven-digit sequential number, the national identity card bears the issuing date and a national identification number assigned to the cardholder under the conditions set out in article 11 … . (Guinea 1995, Art. 6)

According to article 2 of the same Decree, the national identity card must also contain

[translation]

… the cardholder's surname, given name(s), sex, date and place of birth, parentage, place of residence, profession and description. The cardholder's left index fingerprint and signature must be affixed on the card.

A recent photograph of the cardholder, with the head uncovered, as well as a statutory revenue stamp cancelled by the stamp of the authority must also be affixed on it.

… (Guinea 1995)

A copy of a 1998 bulletin establishing the procedure for issuing the national identity card, provided to the Research Directorate in November 2014 correspondence from the Civil Registration Centre for Development (CRC4D) [1] notes that that the police officer responsible for the file laminates the card at the time the card is handed over to the applicant (Guinea 1998).

In November 2014 correspondence with the Research Directorate, a representative from Bao & Fils, a law firm in Conakry that practises corporate law (Bao & Fils n.d.), provided the following information on the physical characteristics of the national identity card and the information it contains:

[translation]

The Guinean national identity card is rectangular in shape and is roughly 9.5 cm long and 6.5 cm wide; the document is fuchsia in colour and contains the following:

On the front: an identification photo of the cardholder taken on a yellow background, two emblems, the stamp of the police station that issued the document, and a space for the cardholder's signature;

On the back: the revenue stamp of 5,000 francs [Guinean francs (GNF)] [C$0.80], the left index fingerprint, one emblem, the stamp and signature of the police commissioner.

…

The identity card includes the following information:

On the front: the cardholder's surname and given name, gender, date and place of birth, parentage and profession.

On the back: [the cardholder's] place of residence, height, skin colour [and] hair colour, as well as the issuing date and location and the expiry date. (Bao & Fils 2014-11-18)

A sample of the paper ID card could not be found among the sources consulted by the Research Directorate within the time constraints of this Response.

3. Requirements and Procedures to Obtain the ID Card Within Guinea, Including Issuance Timeframes

3.1. Biometric ID Card

3.1.1. Requirements and Procedures to Obtain the Biometric ID Card

Joint Order A/2025/235/MATD/MSPC/MAEIAGE/CAB/SGG of 24 March 2025, Establishing the Conditions and Procedures for the Free Issuance of the Biometric National Identity Card (Arrêté conjoint A/2025/235/MATD/MSPC/MAEIAGE/CAB/SGG du 24 mars 2025, portant conditions et modalités d'application de la gratuité de la carte nationale d'ídentité biométrique) provides the following issuing authorities for the biometric ID card and its supporting document:

[translation]

ARTICLE 7

Procedures

The secure digital birth certificate and the certificate of residence shall be issued by the competent divisions of the Ministry responsible for the civil status registry.

The Biometric National Identity Card shall be issued by the competent divisions of the Ministry responsible for Security. … (Guinea 2025b, bold in original)

The same joint order provides the following:

[translation]

ARTICLE 6

Required documents

The issuance of the Biometric National Identity Card is contingent upon the presentation of the following documents, either originals or copies:

- one (1) secure digital birth certificate, issued free of charge;

- one (1) certificate of residence issued within the last three (3) months, provided free of charge. (Guinea 2025b, bold in original)

According to the joint statement by the 4 government ministries, a digital birth certificate extract is required to obtain the biometric national ID card (Guinee7.com 2025-04-12). A 2023 opinion article by a municipal councillor published by Lerevelateur224.com explains that since January 2020, in order to obtain a national ID card an applicant must re-register with the civil registry and obtain a digital birth certificate extract (Diallo 2023-01-20).

The joint statement by the 4 government ministries notes that to obtain the digital birth certificate extract, one of the following documents is required:

  • the old birth certificate extract, or
  • a transcribed suppletory judgment (jugement supplétif), or
  • an identification form from the National Administrative Census Program for the Civil Registry (Programme national de recensement administratif à vocation d'État civil, PN-RAVEC) [2] (Guinee7.com 2025-04-12).

The website of the municipality of N'Zérékoré notes the following required documents to obtain the biometric ID card:

  • the original and a copy of the birth certificate extract or suppletory judgment,
  • a certificate of residence issued by the N'Zérékoré town hall within the last 3 months,
  • 2 [translation] "recent" passport-size photos with a white background and in "biometric format,"
  • a "revenue stamp" paid at the "current rate,"
  • for renewals, the old ID card is also required,
  • for replacement of a lost ID, a declaration of loss is required (N'Zérékoré 2026-06-21).

The 2023 law on the identification of individuals provides the following on the procedure to obtain the biometric ID card:

[translation]

Article 25: Individual and Mandatory Nature of the Secure Biometric National Identity Card

…

For minors, the application for the Secure Biometric National Identity Card shall be submitted by a person exercising parental authority.

For a protected person of full age, the application for the Secure Biometric National Identity Card shall be submitted by the guardian, the supervisory guardian, or the trustee.

However, the person under legal incapacity shall be present when the application for the Secure Biometric National Identity Card is submitted.

In either case, the representative shall provide proof of their status. (Guinea 2023, bold and underline in original)

The 2025 joint order on the conditions and procedures for the free issuance of the biometric national identity card provides the following:

[translation]

ARTICLE 2

Eligible Individuals

The Biometric National Identity Card shall be issued to Guineans aged sixteen (16) and older who are applying for the first time and who meet the applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

ARTICLE 3

Free Issuance

The free issuance of the Biometric National Identity Card shall also include the free issuance of the digital birth certificate, the certificate of residence, and the related photographs.

ARTICLE 4

Exclusion from Free Issuance

The following are not eligible for free issuance of the Biometric National Identity Card:

- citizens under the age of sixteen (16);

- citizens applying for renewal or replacement due to loss, theft, or damage to the card. (Guinea 2025b, bold in original)

The website of the N'Zérékoré municipality describes the following procedure to obtain the biometric ID card:

  1. Prepare the required documents.
  2. Go to the office of the Ministry of Territorial Administration and Decentralization (ministère de l'Administration du territoire et de la Décentralisation, MATD) within the N'Zérékoré municipal office.
  3. Have a biometric photograph and digital fingerprints taken.
  4. Collect the card, once it is ready (N'Zérékoré 2026-06-21).

Quoting the deputy head of the technical department of the city of Kindia [in western Guinea], Lerevelateur224.com indicates that once the applicant has obtained their digitized birth certificate extract, they should go to the police station to collect their biometric ID card; once there, they will receive [translation] "all the necessary information" (2025-04-15). Similarly, Guineematin.com indicates that applicants should go to the police station with their digitized birth certificate extract (2025-04-14b). The same source adds that ID photographs and residency certificates are provided free of charge at civil registry offices and police stations (Guineematin.com 2025-04-14b).

The 2023 law on the identification of individuals provides the following on the renewal of the biometric ID card:

[translation]

Article 27: Renewal of the Secure Biometric National Identity Card

The National Identity Card shall be renewed in the following cases:

  • a change in given names, surname or date of birth;
  • correction of the place of birth and the number of the record of civil status;
  • the correction of parentage;
  • the loss, theft or alteration of the National Identity Card;
  • expiration of the National Identity Card.
  • If state and commune authorities have, pursuant to this Law, accessed data contained in the National Registry of Natural Persons, they shall not further require the production of certificates relating to such data where such data concern individuals whose habitual residence is within the territory of a commune or within the national territory. (Guinea 2023, bold and underline in original)

GuinéeSignal indicates that the fee to renew a biometric ID card is 60,000 GNF [C$9.40] (2026-03-02).

3.1.2. Issuance Timeline and Obstacles to Obtain the Biometric ID Card

Article 7 of the 2025 joint order on the free issuance of the biometric national identity card provides the following: [translation] "The Biometric National Identity Card shall be produced and issued within fifteen (15) days from the date of registration" (Guinea 2025b).

According to Lerevelateur224.com, the biometric ID card should be issued [translation] "at least 2 weeks" after the submission of the application and applicants in N'Zérékoré were told to collect the card 3 months after the application date (2025-11-30). However, media sources report that obtaining the ID card has been a [translation] "headache" for ["many" (Soleil FM 2025-11-19)] citizens, who have had to wait for months after submitting their application (Lerevelateur224.com 2025-11-30; Soleil FM 2025-11-19); some reported having to pay to obtain the card more quickly (Soleil FM 2025-11-19). Similarly, Guinean media sources, citing the National Transitional Council (Conseil national de la transition, CNT) [3], state that in practice, the processing times can sometimes take [translation] "several months"; this has led to "some" officials taking advantage of the situation and charging "'unjustified'" amounts despite the government decree guaranteeing free ID cards to first-time applicants (Guinee7.com 2025-11-18; JournaldeConakry.com 2025-11-18). Quoting a civil registrar from Koundara municipality, Africaguinee.com indicates that [translation] "'[s]ome'" applicants wait 3 to 6 months for their ID cards (2025-12-23).

Regarding the period prior to the introduction of the free issuance of the biometric ID card, the 2021 Guineematin.com article quotes the director responsible for the biometric ID project as stating that once the applicant submits their digital birth certificate extract to the police station, they can collect their biometric ID card after [translation] "'a minimum of two weeks'" (2021-02-03). However, according to a 2023 Guinéenews article, applicants in Labé [in the Fouta Djallon region] faced a [translation] "real ordeal," including having to make multiple trips to different locations to complete different steps of the application procedure over the course of "weeks, if not months" (2023-01-19). A 2023 article by the news website Ledjely.com notes that the registration centres operate at an [translation] "unbelievably slow speed" (2023-02-10). According to the deputy head of the civil registry office for Ratoma municipality, interviewed in 2022 by Africaguinee.com, factors causing the delay include a lack of equipment and applicants submitting fraudulent documents (2022-12-08).

3.2. Paper ID Card

3.2.1. Requirements and Procedures to Obtain the Paper ID Card

According to a report that the government of Guinea and UNICEF commissioned from the CRC4D, the police service, which reports to the Ministry of Security, is responsible for issuing the national identity card (CRC4D 2014-03, 52).

Media sources indicate that to obtain a national ID card in Guinea, the applicant must go to the police station in their region, pay a fee of 15,000 GNF [C$2.36], and provide the following documents: a birth certificate extract, 4 recent photographs, a residence certificate and, if there is any doubt about the applicant's nationality, a nationality certificate (Guinéenews 2017-08-16; Jeune Afrique 2016-04-21; Guinee360 2016-04-21). Sources add that the applicant must appear in person, undergo an interview and also provide a revenue stamp in the amount of 5,000 GNF (Guinéenews 2017-08-16; Jeune Afrique 2016-04-21; Guinee360 2016-04-21).

3.2.2. Issuance Timeline and Obstacles to Obtain the Paper ID Card

Information on the official timelines for issuing Guinean national identity cards could not be found among the sources consulted by the Research Directorate within the time constraints of this Response. Guinean media sources from 2017 note problems encountered by people who have applied for a national identity card in Guinea (Guineematin.com 2017-03-02; Aminata.com 2017-04-19). According to a Guineematin.com correspondent based in Kindia, Guineans who try to obtain a national identity card in that city complain of corruption among police commissioners, unexplained delays, and fees of up to 150,000 GNF [C$23.60] (Guineematin.com 2017-03-02). Sources indicate that in the city of Labé, national identity cards are no longer being issued because of production problems and, according to some citizens, the authorities may require amounts of up to 550,000 GNF [C$86.51] (Aminata.com 2017-04-19; Guineematin.com 2017-07-05).

4. Requirements and Procedures to Obtain the ID Card Abroad

4.1. Biometric ID Card

The news website Guinéejet reports that during a visit to Senegal in April 2026, Guinea's Minister of Foreign Affairs announced the launch of operations to issue biometric ID cards to Guineans residing in Senegal (2026-04-24). The same source adds that [translation] "all" Guinean citizens residing abroad are able to obtain their ID cards at diplomatic missions, which have been equipped to process applications "directly on site" (Guinéejet 2026-04-24).

According to a February 2026 press release by the Consulate General of Guinea in Gambia, the consulate planned to begin enrolling citizens for biometric ID cards and biometric birth certificate extracts on 23 February 2026 (Guinea 2026-02-20). The same source notes that the ID card is issued free of charge and a biometric birth certificate extract or passport and a [translation] "valid consular card" are required (Guinea 2026-02-20). The source further indicates that a copy of the old birth certificate extract or suppletory judgment and a valid consular card are required to obtain the biometric birth certificate extract (Guinea 2026-02-20). Further and corroborating information, including information on the timeline to obtain the biometric ID card from abroad and the implementation of the issuance of the biometric ID card from abroad, could not be found among the sources consulted by the Research Directorate within the time constraints of this Response.

4.2. Paper ID Card

Information on the requirements, procedures, and timeline to obtain the paper ID card from abroad could not be found among the sources consulted by the Research Directorate within the time constraints of this Response.
