​​​​​​​Responses to Information Requests (RIRs) are research reports on country conditions. They are requested by IRB decision-makers.

The database contains a seven-year archive of English and French RIRs. Earlier RIRs may be found on the European Country of Origin Information Network website.

RIR​s published by the IRB on its website may have attachments that are inaccessible due to technical constraints and may include translations of documents originally written in languages other than English or French. To obtain a copy of such attachments and/or translated version of the RIR attachments, please email us.​

Responses to Information Requests (RIRs) cite publicly accessible information available at the time of publication and within time constraints. A list of references and additional sources consulted are included in each RIR. Sources cited are considered the most current information available as of the date of the RIR.            

RIRs are not, and do not purport to be, conclusive as to the merit of any particular claim for refugee protection. Rather, they are intended to support the refugee determination process. More information on the methodology used by the Research Directorate can be found here.          

The assessment and weight to be given to the information in the RIRs are the responsibility of independent IRB members (decision-makers) after considering the evidence and arguments presented by the parties.           

The information presented in RIRs solely reflects the views and perspectives of the sources cited and does not necessarily reflect the position of the IRB or the Government of Canada.          

27 August 2026

MEX202638.E

Mexico: The socio-economic situation, including demographics, employment rates, economic sectors, and crime rates, particularly in Mexico City, Mérida (Yucatán), Puebla City (Puebla), Campeche City (Campeche), and Monterrey (Nuevo León) (2024–August 2026)

Research Directorate, Immigration and Refugee Board of Canada

1. Overview

Figures from the 2020 census, published by Mexico's National Institute of Statistics and Geography (Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Geografía, INEGI), placed the population of Mexico at 126 million (Mexico 2026-06-01). Using data from the same census, Mexico's National Institute of Indigenous Peoples (Instituto Nacional de los Pueblos Indígenas, INPI) indicates that 19.4% of the population self-identified as Indigenous, and 2% of the population self-identified as Afro-Mexican (Mexico n.d.a).

1.1. Socio-Economic Situation

As reported in Bertelsmann Stiftung's Transformation Index (BTI) 2026 [1], Mexico's National Council for Assessing Social Development Policy (Consejo Nacional de Evaluación de la Política de Desarrollo Social, CONEVAL) [2] indicates that the country saw a "significan[t]" poverty rate decrease under the administration of former president López Obrador, from 41.9% to 36.3% (Bertelsmann Stiftung 2026, 18). BTI 2026 adds that, from 2018 to 2024, Mexico's minimum wage went up by 119%, and median wages rose by 10%, "compared with 3% during the previous administration" (Bertelsmann Stiftung 2026, 18−19). The same source describes this increase as "by far the largest in Latin America" (Bertelsmann Stiftung 2026, 19). The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) indicates that while Mexico's "nominal" minimum wage "more than doubled" between January 2021 and July 2026, the country's "real" minimum wage [as adjusted for inflation (Cambridge Dictionary n.d.)] grew by 68% during the same period (2026-07-07, 1). The same source adds that the real minimum wage rose by 8.2% between April 2025 and April 2026, "one of the strongest increases … across the OECD" (OECD 2026-07-07, 1).

The following table, taken from BTI 2026, presents national economic indicators for the years 2021−2024:

Economic indicators 2021 2022 2023 2024
GDP $M [3] 1,316,569.5 1,466,464.9 1,793,798.9 1,852,722.9
GDP growth % 6.0 3.7 3.3 1.5
Inflation % 5.7 7.9 5.5 4.7
Unemployment % 4.0 3.3 2.8 2.7

(Bertelsmann Stiftung 2026, 19, bold in original).

According to June 2026 figures published by INEGI, the national unemployment rate is 2.90% (Mexico 2026-06-01).

BTI 2026 notes that Mexico's employment rate for women (39.5%) was the lowest among Latin American and Caribbean countries according to 2023 figures from the World Bank (Bertelsmann Stiftung 2026, 25). The same source notes that these figures do not capture the rate of women's participation in informal employment (Bertelsmann Stiftung 2026, 25). According to the OECD, women in Mexico complete twice as many hours per week of unpaid domestic and care work (40) compared to men; as a result, "many" of them work informal jobs "that provide flexibility but offer little security, few benefits, and limited prospects for career development" (2026-02, 63).

1.2. Crime Situation

Canada's travel advice for Mexico indicates that the country has high levels of crime across the country, particularly violent crime (Canada 2026-06-23). According to the Institute for Economics & Peace (IEP) [4], in 2025 Mexico recorded its "most substantial improvement in peacefulness in at least a decade," with a 5.3% drop in the national violent crime rate, and 22 of 32 Mexican states registering improvements in their peace scores (2026-05, ii, 2). The most peaceful [5] state was Yucatán for the ninth consecutive year, followed by Chiapas, Tlaxcala, Durango, and Campeche; Puebla, Mexico City, and Nuevo León ranked 13th, 19th, and 21st, respectively (IEP 2026-05, 2, 7). The IEP further notes that Nuevo León was among the 5 states with the largest improvements in peacefulness in 2024−2025, while Mexico City was among those with the largest deteriorations (2026-05, 13).

The information in the following paragraph was provided by the Global Initiative Against Transnational Organized Crime (GI-TOC), a civil society organization based in Geneva (GI-TOC n.d.), in its 2025 crime index for Mexico:

Mexico continues to be a "significant hub" for human trafficking due to economic inequality, violence, migration, and "weak" institutional structures. Extortion in the country has reached a "pervasive" level, "affecting businesses, agriculture, unions and public officials" in "at least 30 of Mexico's 32 states." Illegal arms trade in the country and across the border with the US continues to "drive violence in Mexico, contributing to high homicide rates," and facilitating organized crime networks and activities like "human trafficking, extortion, kidnapping and armed robbery." Finally, "[m]any" of Mexico's municipal law enforcement agencies are "infiltrated" by organized crime, making it challenging to reduce cartel influence (GI-TOC 2025-11-10, 3, 5).

The Citizens' National Observatory (Observatorio Nacional Ciudadano, ONC) [6], citing data from Mexico's Executive Secretariat of the National Public Security System (Secretariado Ejecutivo del Sistema Nacional de Seguridad Pública, SESNSP), reports the following crime statistics for Mexico:

April–June 2025 April–June 2026
Crime rate (per 100,000 population) 385.24 386.30
Intentional homicide rate (per 100,000 population) 4 2.97

(ONC 2026-06a).

The same source notes that, in Mexico as a whole, the most common types of crime in the second quarter of 2026 were

  • domestic violence (55.25 cases per 100,000 inhabitants),
  • causing bodily harm with intent (31.99 per 100,000 inhabitants), and
  • robbery (23.56 per 100,000 inhabitants) (ONC 2026-06a).

For further information on the crime situation in Mexico, including organized crime, see Response to Information Request MEX201993 of September 2024.

2. Mexico City

2.1. Demographics

Data México, an online data visualization platform administered by Mexico's Ministry of Economy that uses statistics from various government databases (Mexico n.d.b), reports that Mexico City's population was approximately 9.2 million according to 2020 census data (Mexico n.d.c). The population of the city's metropolitan area was approximately 21.8 million (Mexico n.d.d).

According to the 2020 INEGI census, 9.2% of Mexico City's population aged 3 years and above [translation] "self-identifies as Indigenous" (Mexico 2023, 44). In a sociodemographic overview of Mexico City using the results of the 2020 INEGI census, INEGI indicates that 2.05% of the population [translation] "self-identifies as Afro-Mexican, Black, or Afro-descendant" (Mexico 2021, 8).

2.2. Employment Rates and Economic Sectors

Citing figures for the first quarter of 2026 from Mexico's National Survey on Occupation and Employment (Encuesta Nacional de Ocupación y Empleo, ENOE), Data México reports that Mexico City's unemployment rate was 3.98% and the average monthly salary of its residents, including informal workers, was 7,490 Mexican pesos (MXN) [C$610] (Mexico n.d.c). The most common occupations in the city are "Sales Employees, Dispatchers and [Those] Dependent on Trade," "Traders in Stores," and "Support Workers in Various Administrative Activities" (Mexico n.d.c).

According to the OECD, the skill level of Mexico City's workforce is "high," correlating with a relatively lower rate of informal employment (45%) in 2024 (2026-02, 59). According to 2024 figures published by INEGI, 19.7% of Mexico City's residents were living in poverty [7], which is below the national average of 29.6%; 1.8% were living in "extreme" poverty [8], below the country average of 5.3% (Mexico 2025a, 5).

2.3. Crime Rates

INEGI's 2025 National Survey of Victimization and Perception of Public Safety (Encuesta Nacional de Victimización y Percepción sobre Seguridad Pública, ENVIPE) found that 49.4% of Mexico City residents aged 18 and above felt that living in their neighbourhood was [translation] "unsafe," compared with 47.5% in 2024 (Mexico 2025-09, 30). The same survey indicates that 29.6% of women and 46% of men aged 18 and above felt [translation] "safe" walking alone in their neighbourhood at night (Mexico 2025-09, 31).

According to the IEP, the city "consistently" registers robbery rates that are "among the top three worst" in the country (2026-05, 27). The same source states, however, that 2 of Mexico City's 16 boroughs, namely Benito Juárez and Coyoacán, were among the country's municipalities with the lowest homicide rates in 2025 (IEP 2026-05, 21, 22). According to INEGI, 4 of Mexico City's boroughs—Iztapalapa (47.5%), Tláhuac (46.9%), Xochimilco (45.6%), and Gustavo A. Madero (45.4%)—topped the list of municipalities nationwide with the [translation] "most" households where a member was a victim of "theft/robbery, extortion and/or fraud" in the second quarter of 2025 (Mexico 2026-01-23, 25−27, footnote omitted). The rates for the other 12 municipalities of Mexico City were as follows:

  • Iztacalco: 42.2%
  • Azcapotzalco: 40.4%
  • Milpa Alta: 39.4%
  • Benito Juárez: 36.9%
  • La Magdalena Contreras: 36.5%
  • Cuauhtémoc: 35.2%
  • Tlalpan: 34.4%
  • Miguel Hidalgo: 32.7%
  • Coyoacán: 32.1%
  • Álvaro Obregón: 30.7%
  • Venustiano Carranza: 22.1%
  • Cuajimalpa de Morelos: 20.3% (Mexico 2026-01-23, 25−26).

Citing figures from SESNSP, the ONC provides the following crime statistics for Mexico City:

April–June 2025 April–June 2026
Crime rate (per 100,000 population) 595.90 603.41
Intentional homicide rate (per 100,000 population) 2.29 2.25

(ONC 2026-06b).

The same source notes that the most common types of crime in the second quarter of 2026 were

  • domestic violence (78.59 per 100,000 inhabitants),
  • street theft/mugging (40.83 per 100,000 inhabitants), and
  • robbery (29.23 per 100,000 inhabitants) (ONC 2026-06b).

3. Mérida

3.1. Demographics

According to Data México, census data from 2020 placed Mérida's population at approximately 1 million (Mexico n.d.e). The city's metropolitan area had a population of approximately 1.3 million (Mexico n.d.f). INEGI's 2020 census data reported by the INPI indicates that 18.2% of Mérida's population self-identified as Indigenous, and 2.8% of the population self-identified as Afro-Mexican (Mexico 2020).

3.2. Employment Rates and Economic Sectors

Citing ENOE figures from the first quarter of 2026, Data México indicates that the state of Yucatán had an unemployment rate of 1.55% and the average monthly salary of its residents was 7,700 MXN [C$621] (Mexico n.d.e). The most common occupations were "Sales Employees, Dispatchers and [Those] Dependent on Trade," "Bricklayers, Stonemasons and Related," and "Domestic Workers" (Mexico n.d.e).

According to the OECD, the skill level of the state of Yucatán's workforce is "intermediate high," correlating with a relatively lower rate of informal employment (59%) in 2024 (2026-02, 59). According to 2024 figures published by INEGI, 26.6% of Yucatán residents were living in poverty, which was lower than the national average of 29.6%; 3.6% of residents were living in "extreme" poverty, below the country average of 5.3% (Mexico 2025b, 5).

3.3. Crime Rates

INEGI indicates that in 2024, 61.1% of Yucatán residents aged 18 and above felt safe walking alone in their neighbourhood at night, which was above the national average of 41.3%; in 2023, that same statistic was 57.8% for Yucatán (Mexico 2025b, 29). According to ENSU data, 36.9% of Mérida residents felt that [translation] "living in their city was … unsafe" as of December 2025, compared to 31.5% in December 2024 (Mexico 2026-01-23, 3, 7). The same survey notes that 32.8% of households in Mérida had a member experience a [translation] "theft/robbery, extortion and/or fraud" in the second quarter of 2025 (Mexico 2026-01-23, 26).

The IEP reports that the state of Yucatán has the lowest rate of organized crime in Mexico, with levels 11 times lower than the national average (2026-05, 25). According to the IEP, Mérida has the lowest homicide rate of any state capital with a rate of 1.6 per 100,000 people (2026-05, 22).

Citing SESNSP figures, the ONC provides the following crime statistics for Mérida

April–June 2025 April–June 2026
Crime rate (per 100,000 population) 70.51 111.84
Intentional homicide rate (per 100,000 population)) 0.59 0.39

(ONC 2026-06c).

The same source indicates that the most common types of crime in the second quarter of 2026 were

  • causing bodily harm with intent (9.62 per 100,000 inhabitants),
  • domestic violence (6.12 per 100,000 inhabitants), and
  • culpable homicide (3.21 per 100,000 inhabitants) (ONC 2026-06c).

4. Puebla City

4.1. Demographics

Census data from 2020 reported by Data México placed Puebla City's population at approximately 1.7 million (Mexico n.d.g) and the metropolitan area's population at approximately 3.2 million (Mexico n.d.h).

INEGI census data reported by the INPI indicates that 6.6% of Puebla City's population self-identified as Indigenous, and 2.3% of the population self-identified as Afro-Mexican (Mexico 2020).

4.2. Employment Rates and Economic Sectors

As reported by Data México, ENOE figures from the first quarter of 2026 indicate that the state of Puebla has an unemployment rate of 1.8% and the average monthly salary of its residents is 5,120 MXN [C$412] (Mexico n.d.g). The most common occupations are "Support Workers in Agriculture," "Sales Employees, Dispatchers and [Those] Dependent on Trade," and "Traders in Stores" (Mexico n.d.g).

According to the OECD, the skill level of the state of Puebla's workforce is "intermediate low," correlating with a relatively higher rate of informal employment (69%) in 2024 (2026-02, 59). According to 2024 figures published by INEGI, 43.4% of Puebla state's residents were living in poverty, which was higher than the national average of 29.6%; 7.3% were living in "extreme" poverty, which was above the national average of 5.3% (Mexico 2025c, 5).

4.3. Crime Rates

INEGI indicates that in 2024, 39% of Puebla state residents aged 18 and above felt safe walking alone in their neighbourhood at night, which was below the national average of 41.3%; in 2023, that statistic for Puebla state was 34.3% (Mexico 2025c, 29). According to figures from ENSU, 84.5% of Puebla City residents felt that [translation] "living in their city was … unsafe" as of December 2025, compared to 75.5% in December 2024 (Mexico 2026-01-23, 3, 7).

Citing SESNSP figures, the ONC provides the following crime statistics for Puebla City:

April–June 2025 April–June 2026
Crime rate (per 100,000 population) 495.99 516.77
Intentional homicide rate (per 100,000 population)) 2.66 2.30

(ONC 2026-06d).

The same source indicates that the most common types of crime in the second quarter of 2026 were

  • robbery (67.33 per 100,000 inhabitants),
  • street theft/mugging (62.96 per 100,000 inhabitants), and
  • domestic violence (51.74 per 100,000 inhabitants) (ONC 2026-06d).

5. Campeche City

5.1. Demographics

Data México indicates, citing 2020 census data, that the city of Campeche had a population of approximately 294,000 (Mexico n.d.i).

INEGI census data reported by the INPI indicates that 12% of Campeche City's population self-identified as Indigenous, and 2.3% of the population self-identified as Afro-Mexican in 2020 (Mexico 2020).

5.2. Employment Rates and Economic Sectors

As reported by Data México, citing ENOE figures, the state of Campeche had an unemployment rate of 2.79% in the first quarter of 2026, and the average monthly salary of its residents was 8,390 MXN [C$675] (Mexico n.d.i). The most common occupations were "Workers in the Corn and/or Bean [Sector]," "Sales Employees, Dispatchers and [Those] Dependent on Trade," and "Traders in Stores" (Mexico n.d.i).

According to the OECD, the skill level of the state of Campeche's workforce is "intermediate low," correlating with a relatively higher rate of informal employment (60%) in 2024 (2026-02, 59). According to 2024 figures published by INEGI, 36.7% of Campeche state residents were living in poverty, which was higher than the national average of 29.6%; 5.8% were living in "extreme" poverty, which was above the country average of 5.3% (Mexico 2025d, 5).

5.3. Crime Rates

INEGI data indicates that in 2024, 47.8% of Campeche state residents aged 18 and above felt safe walking alone in their neighbourhood at night, which was higher than the national average of 41.3%; in 2023, that statistic was 43.9% for Campeche state (Mexico 2025d, 29). According to figures from ENSU, 62.6% of Campeche city residents felt that [translation] "living in their city was … unsafe" as of December 2025, compared to 48.8% in December 2024 (Mexico 2026-01-23, 3, 7).

Citing SESNSP data, the ONC provides the following crime statistics for the city of Campeche:

April–June 2025 April–June 2026
Crime rate (per 100,000 population) 142.27 121.25
Intentional homicide rate (per 100,000 population)) 1.75 0.86

(ONC 2026-06e).

The same source indicates that the most common types of crime in the second quarter of 2026 were

  • drug dealing (14.91 per 100,000),
  • theft from businesses (13.47 per 100,000), and
  • causing bodily harm with intent (10.61 per 100,000) (ONC 2026-06e).
  • Campeche City, according to the IEP, has one of the lowest homicide rates among Mexico's state capitals (2026-05, 22).

6. Monterrey

6.1. Demographics

Data México reports that, as of the 2020 census, Monterrey had a population of approximately 1.1 million (Mexico n.d.j), while the population of the city's metropolitan area was approximately 5.3 million (Mexico n.d.k).

INEGI census data reported by the INPI indicates that 1.9% of Monterrey's population self-identified as Indigenous, and 1.8% of the population self-identified as Afro-Mexican (Mexico 2020).

6.2. Employment Rates and Economic Sectors

Citing first quarter 2026 figures from ENOE, Data México reports that the state of Nuevo León's unemployment rate was 2.82% and the average monthly salary of its residents was 9,250 MXN [C$753] (Mexico n.d.j). The most common occupations were "Traders in Stores," "Sales Employees, Dispatchers and [Those] Dependent on Trade," and "[Drivers of] Truck[s]…, Cargo Vans and Cars" (Mexico n.d.j).

According to the OECD, the skill level of Nuevo León's workforce is "high," correlating with a relatively lower rate of informal employment (35%) in 2024 (2026-02, 59). According to 2024 figures published by INEGI, 10.6% of Nuevo León residents were living in poverty, which was below the national average of 29.6%; 0.5% were living in "extreme" poverty, compared to 5.3% in Mexico as a whole (Mexico 2025e, 5).

6.3. Crime Rates

INEGI indicates that in 2024, 36% of Nuevo León residents aged 18 and above felt safe walking alone in their neighbourhood at night, which is below the national average of 41.3%; in 2023, that statistic was 36.5% for Nuevo León (Mexico 2025e, 29). According to ENSU, 62.6% of adult Monterrey residents felt that [translation] "living in their city was … unsafe" as of December 2025, compared to 69.2% in December 2024 (Mexico 2026-01-23, 3, 7).

Citing SESNSP figures, the ONC provides the following crime statistics for Monterrey:

April–June 2025 April–June 2026
Crime rate (per 100,000 population) 407.86 451.03
Intentional homicide rate (per 100,000 population)) 3.91 2.21

(ONC 2026-06f).

The same source indicates that the most common types of crime in the second quarter of 2026 were

  • domestic violence (97.61 per 100,000),
  • drug dealing (79.64 per 100,000), and
  • causing bodily harm with intent (18.46 per 100,000) (ONC 2026-06f).

Canada's travel advice for Mexico advises against non-essential travel to the state of Nuevo León except for the city of Monterrey (Canada 2026-06-23).
