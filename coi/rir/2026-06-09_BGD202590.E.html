Responses to Information Requests

Page Content

​​​​​​​Responses to Information Requests (RIRs) are research reports on country conditions. They are requested by IRB decision-makers.

The database contains a seven-year archive of English and French RIRs. Earlier RIRs may be found on the European Country of Origin Information Network website.

RIR​s published by the IRB on its website may have attachments that are inaccessible due to technical constraints and may include translations of documents originally written in languages other than English or French. To obtain a copy of such attachments and/or translated version of the RIR attachments, please email us.​

Disclaimer

Responses to Information Requests (RIRs) cite publicly accessible information available at the time of publication and within time constraints. A list of references and additional sources consulted are included in each RIR. Sources cited are considered the most current information available as of the date of the RIR.            

RIRs are not, and do not purport to be, conclusive as to the merit of any particular claim for refugee protection. Rather, they are intended to support the refugee determination process. More information on the methodology used by the Research Directorate can be found here.          

The assessment and weight to be given to the information in the RIRs are the responsibility of independent IRB members (decision-makers) after considering the evidence and arguments presented by the parties.           

The information presented in RIRs solely reflects the views and perspectives of the sources cited and does not necessarily reflect the position of the IRB or the Government of Canada.          

9 June 2026

BGD202590.E

Bangladesh: Certified copies of police and court-related documents, including summonses, warrants, First Information Reports (FIRs), Police General Diaries (GD), charge sheets, and magistrate's orders; requirements and procedures to obtain them from police and courts from within the country and abroad; whether an application date, stamped or handwritten, appears on the requested copies; whether there is a publicly accessible registry of indictments, arrest warrants, and wanted notices (2024–May 2026)

Research Directorate, Immigration and Refugee Board of Canada

1. Overview

Tahmidur Remura Wahid (TRW), a law firm headquartered in Bangladesh whose practice areas include criminal law (TRW n.d.), states that a certified copy of a court document is an "official copy issued by the court, bearing the court's seal and the certifying officer's signature" (2026-05-07). In correspondence with the Research Directorate, an associate professor of law at Notre Dame University Bangladesh who has published articles on criminal law in the country, noted that procedures to obtain certified true copies of police and court-related documents are governed by the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898, the Evidence Act 1872, and the Criminal Rules and Orders (Associate Professor of Law 2026-05-03). A copy of the Criminal Rules and Orders could not be found among the sources consulted by the Research Directorate within the time constraints of this Response.

When asked if an individual can request a certified copy of an FIR, charge sheet, police report, and complaint petition, a lawyer and senior lecturer of law at the Independent University, Bangladesh, pointed to Section 173(4) of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898, noting that the accused can make an application to request all 4 documents (Senior Lecturer 2026-04-04). For information on FIRs, see Response to Information Request BGD200855 of January 2022. For information on charge sheets, police reports and complaint petitions, see Response to Information Request BGD202563 of June 2026.

The Evidence Act, 1872 provides the following on certified copies of public documents:

74. The following documents are public documents:–

(1) documents forming the acts or records of the acts-

(i) of the sovereign authority,

(ii) of official bodies and tribunals, and

(iii) of public officers, legislative, judicial and executive of any part of Bangladesh or of the Commonwealth, or of a foreign country;

(2) public records kept in Bangladesh of private documents

75. All other documents are private.

76. Every public officer having the custody of a public document, which any person has a right to inspect, shall give that person on demand a copy of it on payment of the legal fees therefor, together with a certificate written at the foot of such copy that it is a true copy of such document or part thereof, as the case may be, and such certificate shall be dated and subscribed by such officer with his name and his official title, and shall be sealed, whenever such officer is authorized by law to make use of a seal, and such copies so certified shall be called certified copies.

Explanation.-Any officer who, by the ordinary course of official duty, is authorized to deliver such copies, shall be deemed to have the custody of such documents within the meaning of this section. (Bangladesh 1872)

Article 173 of the Code of the Criminal Procedure, 1898 provides the following:

173.(1) Every investigation under this Chapter shall be completed without unnecessary delay, and, as soon as it is completed, the officer in charge of the police-station shall-

(a) forward to a Magistrate empowered to take cognizance of the offence on a police-report a report, in the form prescribed by the Government, setting forth the names of the parties, the nature of the information and the names of the persons who appear to be acquainted with the circumstances of the case, and stating whether the accused (if arrested) has been forwarded in custody or has been released on his bond, and, if so, whether with or without sureties, and

…

[(3A) When such report is in respect of a case to which section 170 applies, the police-officer shall forward to the Magistrate along with the report-

(a) all documents or relevant extracts thereof on which the prosecution proposes to rely other than those already sent to the Magistrate during investigation;

(b) the statements recorded under sub-section (3) of section 161 of all the persons whom the prosecution proposes to examine as its witnesses.

…

(4) a copy of any report forwarded under this section shall on application, be furnished to the accused before the commencement of the inquiry or trial:

Provided that the same shall be paid for unless the Magistrate for some special reason thinks fit to furnish it free of cost.

… (Bangladesh 1898, footnotes omitted and square bracket in original)

2. Requirements and Procedure to Obtain Certified Copies Within Bangladesh

2.1 Eligible Applicants

Legal sources indicate that "[g]enerally, only parties to the case" can apply to obtain certified copies (TRW 2026-05-07) or that parties to the case have an "inherent right" to such copies (Associate Professor of Law 2026-05-03). However, the same sources add that third parties "with a legitimate interest" can apply "in some circumstances" (TRW 2026-05-07) or that third parties "may be required to demonstrate sufficient interest or seek permission" (Associate Professor of Law 2026-05-03).

Chapter XIII of the Supreme Court of Bangladesh (High Court Division) Rules, 1973, provides the following on the rules and rights to obtain copies:

  1. Right of appearing party to obtain copy.- A plaintiff or a defendant who has appeared at any stage [is entitled] to obtain a copy of the record of the suit, including exhibits which have been put in and finally accepted by the Court as evidence.
  2. Copy allowable to stranger after decree.-A stranger to a suit may, after decree, obtain a copy of the plaint [,] written statements, affidavits, and petitions filed in the suit, and may, for sufficient reasons shown to the satisfaction of the Registrar, obtain [***] a copy of any such document before decree.
  3. Copy of judgment etc. to stranger.-A stranger to a suit may also obtain a copy of any judgment. decree, or order at any time after it has been passed or made.
  4. No copy of exhibits to stranger without party's consent.-A stranger to a suit has no right to obtain a copy of exhibits admitted in evidence, except with the consent of the person by whom they were produced or his successor-in-interest.
    1. He may obtain copies of other documents in which he has an interest including depositions for bonafide use in Courts and case-maps, at any time after they have been proved.
  5. Advocate's right to obtain copy.-Every Advocate engaged in any case shall be entitled to obtain from the Court's office a copy of the whole or any part of the proceedings and evidence in the case on depositing the estimated cost of such copy. (Bangladesh 1973, emphasis and square brackets in original, footnotes omitted)

2.2. Certified Copies from the Courts

According to the Associate Professor of Law, in order to obtain certified copies, a written application specifying the case number, parties involved, and documents requested must be submitted to the Nakalkhana (copying section) of the relevant court (2026-05-03). The Senior Lecturer noted that certified copies of court documents are requested through a formal application by the lawyer of the accused (2026-04-04).

In correspondence with the Research Directorate, an associate professor of criminology at Mawlana Bhashani Science and Technology University in Bangladesh, who is also a PhD student in legal studies at Carleton University and who has conducted research on the criminal justice process in Bangladesh, stated that certified copies of documents are obtained through court procedures, but that access is mediated by "legal intermediaries" such as defence attorneys or authorized representatives (Associate Professor of Criminology 2026-03-30). The source added, however, that access is "uneven in practice" since the process involves "multiple administrative steps," and "procedural delays and bureaucratic inefficiencies are common" (Associate Professor of Criminology 2026-03-30).

Dhaka Tribune, a daily newspaper in Bangladesh, notes that there are "persistent red tape and corruption" in the judiciary (2025-11-10). Australia's Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) states that court bureaucracy is "slow" and employees "demand bribes" to move documents or to complete "simples processes"; however, it adds that it is "possible" to obtain documents if one pays the price, which includes both "fees and bribes" (Australia 2025-07-23, para. 5.12).

The Associate Professor of Law noted that FIRs can be obtained from the courts once they become a "part of the judicial record" or from the General Register Office at the police station (2026-05-03). The same source added that charge sheets and magistrate's orders can be obtained from the court, once they have been filed, and copies of summons and warrants can be obtained from the copying section of the issuing court (Associate Professor of Law 2026-05-03).

According to TRW, to obtain certified copies from the Supreme Court, including the Appellate Division and High Court Division, an application must be sent to the copying section of the Supreme Court; while the procedure for the lower courts is "similar," there may be differences in the fee and waiting time (2026-05-07). The source notes the following procedure to obtain certified copies:

  • submit the application for a certified copy to the relevant court, specifying the requested document and the case number;
  • pay the court fee, which is calculated based on the number of pages and type of document requested;
  • wait for the court's copying section to process the application, with an "ordinary" application taking 7−15 days and an "urgent" application taking 2−3;
  • collect the certified copy (TRW 2026-05-07).

Chapter XIII of the Supreme Court (High Court Division) Rules, 1973 provides the following on the requirements and procedures to obtain certified copies:

[38. Certified Photostat copy.-(1) Persons entitled to obtain copies of papers, records, Judgments, Decrees, Orders, exhibits and other documents under rules 1 to 5 [and 12] of this Chapter may obtain photostat copies of the same subject to the fulfillment of the terms and conditions, and in the manner specified below.

(2) For such photostat copies offset paper shall be used. The applicant shall, within one week of the notification of the estimate by the office, supply the required number of sheet, and pay the fees under sub-rule (5). The supply of the paper and the payment of the fees shall be entered, in the relevant Register.

(3) If the applicant fails to supply the estimated number of papers or to pay the requisite fees within the period of one week of the notification under sub-rule (2) the application shall be rejected by the [concerned Assistant Registrar].

(4) An application for a photostat copy shall be made in Form No. 24A (civil), [Appendix 1] which will be supplied on payment of [Tk.4/-] [4 Bangladeshi Taka (BDT) C$0.04] per form in Court-fee stamp.

[(5) The following fees, payable in Court fee stamps, shall be chargeable for photostat copies:-

Taka
(I) Application fee (Ordinary) 10/ - [C$0.11]
(II) Application fee (urgent) 15/ - [C$0.16]
(III) Charges of copies per page 3/ - [C$0.03]
(IV) Authentication fee for judgement 5/ - [C$0.05]
(V) Authentication fee for decrees 10/ -
(VI) Authentication fee for other documents 5/ -
(VII) Searching fee, if the record is in deposit in the Record Section 5/ -

(Note-Court fee stamps realized as charge fee copies shall be affixed on each page of the copy.)]

(6) A copy shall be supplied:-

  1. [against] an ordinary application within one week: and
  2. [against] an urgent application, within three days, of the filling of the papers and the payment of the requisite fees. (Bangladesh 1973, footnotes omitted and square brackets in original, excepting currency conversion)

A template of Form No.24A (civil) used to make an application for photostat copies is available in Appendix I of the Supreme Court (High Court Division) Rules, 1973, which is available online (Bangladesh 1973).

2.2.1. Regional Differences

According to the Associate Professor of Law, while the procedure for obtaining certified true copies is set out in law "uniformly nationwide," variation exists due to regional differences "in administrative capacity and digitization" (2026-05-03). For example, "major" cities such as Dhaka and Chattogram have "more efficient" copying sections (Associate Professor of Law 2026-05-03). Corroborating information could not be found among the sources consulted by the Research Directorate within the time constraints of this Response.

2.3. Obtaining Certified Copies from the Police

Information on the requirements and procedures to obtain certified copies from the police could not be found among the sources consulted by the Research Directorate within the time constraints of this Response.

The Associate Professor of Law stated that certified copies of FIRs can be obtained from the General Register Office at police stations, although they can be "more reliably" obtained from the courts (2026-05-03). The same source indicated that certified copies of GD "must be" requested from the police and are "subject to discretion," "often requiring" the applicant to provide proof of "legitimate interest" in such records (Associate Professor of Law 2026-05-03). Corroborating information could not be found among the sources consulted by the Research Directorate within the time constraints of this Response.

For information on the GD, see Response to Information Request BGD202346 of July 2025.

3. Requirements and Procedure to Obtain Certified Copies from Abroad

According to the Associate Professor of Law, there is no online application to request certified copies from abroad (2026-05-03). The source added that applying for true certified documents from abroad "typically require[s] an authorized representative," such as a lawyer or a relative (Associate Professor of Law 2026-05-03). While a vakalatnama (power of attorney) is "often" required for court processes, a "general power of attorney or authorization letter may suffice" for "other" matters (Associate Professor of Law 2026-05-03). Corroborating information could not be found among the sources consulted by the Research Directorate within the time constraints of this Response.

4. Appearance of Certified Copies

According to the Associate Professor of Law, certified copies do not include an application date (2026-05-03). Corroborating information could not be found among the sources consulted by the Research Directorate within the time constraints of this Response.

Chapter XIII of the Supreme Court (High Court Division) Rules 1973 provides the following on the appearance of certified copies:

[36.Manner of certifying a copy.- (1) Certified copies shall bear the seal of the Court and a certificate with words ''Certified to be a true copy as per section 76, Act I of 1872" / "আইন ১/১৮৭২ এর ৭৬ ধারা অনুযায়ী প্রত্যায়িত অবিকল অনুলিপি" and be signed in full by an officer authorised to do so by the Registrar.

2) As regards uncertified copies they shall be marked only with the word "examined" and initialled by the Examiner.] (Bangladesh 1973, footnotes omitted and square brackets in original)

The same document provides the following:

6.Copy to be certified.-Every such copy shall be examined and certified as correct [by the concerned Assistant Registrar] before it is issued from the Court.

[7. Manner of preparing copy.-(1) Copies shall be prepared and delivered to an applicant either in sheets of offset paper of 100 gram, or in folios/stamp sheet sold by Government authorities for this purpose.

…

(3) Where a copy is prepared in offset legal size paper each sheet shall contain not more than 18 lines. and the following vacant space shall be maintained: -

(i) 2.50" on the top:

(ii) 2" on the left and 2" on the right margin;

(iii) 2.50" at the bottom.

(4) Every sheet of folio shall contain the following vacant space:-

(i) 2.50"/or the Government mark of the stamp:

(ii) 2" on the left and 2"on the right side;

(iii) 2.50" at the bottom.

(5) The text of computer print of a copy shall ordinarily be 14 pt in font size in case of English and 12 pt in case of Bangla. There may be variation in case of capital letters or in case of a Table etc.

(6) Each sheet of a copy shall be initialled by the concerned Assistant Registrar. The last page shall be singed [sic] by the person(s) preparing and comparing the copy with their full name and designation. The last page must contain a certificate with the expression “Certified to be true copy as per section 76, Act I of 1872 or "আইন ১/১৮৭২ এর ৭৬ ধারা অনুযায়ী প্রত্যায়িত অবিকল অনুলিপি" under which the full name and designation of the officer shall be mentioned with space for signature. (Bangladesh 1973, footnotes omitted and square brackets in original)

5. Publicly Accessible Registry

According to the Associate Professor of Law, there is no "centralized" online registry listing indictments, arrest warrants, or criminal suspects, although the Bangladesh Police "occasionally publishes a 'limited' wanted list" with a select group of "fugitives" (2026-05-03). A "List of Most Wanted," which includes photos of the accused, as well as their names and addresses, is available on the website of the Bangladesh Police (Bangladesh n.d.).

A database of public "Red Notices" is available on the website of INTERPOL, which is searchable by the nationality of the wanted individual, among other options (INTERPOL n.d.). According to a search of the database conducted by the Research Directorate selecting Bangladesh nationality, search results include the accused's photo, name, and age, along with country of citizenship (INTERPOL n.d.). INTERPOL distinguishes a Red Notice, a "request to law enforcement worldwide to locate and provisionally arrest a person pending extradition, surrender, or similar legal actions," from an "international arrest warrant"; information on Red Notices is "published at the request of the member country" (n.d.).
