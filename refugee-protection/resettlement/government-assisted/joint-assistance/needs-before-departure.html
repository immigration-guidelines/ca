Joint assistance sponsorship (JAS): Exceptional needs identified before departure for Canada

(REF-OVS-2-4-1)

This section contains policy, procedures and guidance used by IRCC staff. It is posted on the department’s website as a courtesy to stakeholders.

A government-assisted refugee (GAR) case may be suitable for the Joint Assistance Sponsorship (JAS) Program if the refugee’s need for settlement assistance is greater than that of other GAR cases. These instructions provide information on the processing stages when such needs are identified before the refugee’s resettlement to Canada.

Key messages: A case that is referred before the refugee arrives in Canada remains overseas while the JAS profile is promoted to sponsors. If the case is not matched with a sponsor, it remains a GAR case, and the refugee travels to Canada.

  • Referring a case to the JAS Program
  • Matching with a sponsor

Referring a case to the JAS program

Migration officers identify and assess cases that may benefit from the JAS Program because of exceptional resettlement needs that may include

  • a physical or mental health condition that could require treatment in Canada
  • a complex family configuration, such as a single-parent family with several young children, or a family consisting only of siblings, where one or more have assumed parental responsibilities
  • other special needs identified by a migration officer

Note: Sponsor support is not intended to replace specialized support services and some cases (for example those with high mental-health needs) are not suited to matching with a sponsoring group because groups are volunteer-based.

Officers in the Resettlement Operations Centre in Ottawa (ROC-O) may also identify suitable JAS cases at the Destination Matching Request (DMR) stage. If the case notes show no indication of a JAS assessment, the ROC-O officer will communicate with the migration office to clarify whether a JAS assessment has been conducted and will request an assessment, if warranted.

If a refugee being considered for the JAS program is in need of urgent protection, officers should consult the “Referrals for urgent protection” section of the Resettlement: Cases for priority or special processing page.

Releasing refugee information

Authorization to release information about the refugee to potential sponsoring groups must be obtained from the applicant before an officer submits the referral to ROC-O.

Migration officers ask JAS-referred applicants to sign part D of Schedule 2 of the IMM008 form or the IMM 5475 Authority to Release to Designated Persons if the required form is not already signed.

Migration officers should try to release all available information that is relevant to the sponsorship, such as family size and composition, Canadian relatives and friends, education, work experience, and even general information on traumatic experiences the refugee has endured (for example, torture, long-term incarceration).

Note: Officers are asked to place an asterisk (*) next to any information that should not be released to a sponsor. This may include information that the refugee has asked to be kept confidential as long as it would not affect the sponsor’s decision, or specific details about traumatic experiences. For example, the refugee may wish to withhold the fact that they are a survivor of sexual violence but not the fact that trauma counselling may be needed.

Migration officer submits JAS referral and any other applicable attachments

The officer completes the JAS referral template and submits it to ROC-O along with any medical and resettlement needs information. Before submitting the case to ROC-O, the officer ensures that all medical, security and criminality screenings have been completed.

Matching with a sponsor

Once suitability has been confirmed, ROC-O prepares a case profile that is shared with the Refugee Sponsorship Training Program (RSTP) for promotion and matching with potential sponsoring groups. RSTP promotes the case for a maximum of 4 months; a short-term extension may be granted in exceptional circumstances.

Only sponsorship agreement holders and their constituent groups in provinces and territories outside Quebec are eligible to participate in the JAS program. Groups of 5 and community sponsors are not eligible.

Interested sponsors submit the completed sponsorship undertaking form (IMM 1324), the Sponsor Assessment form (IMM 5492) and the Settlement Plan form (IMM 5494) (if required) to ROC-O for approval.

Making a final decision

ROC-O assesses the submitted information and the information on file to determine if the sponsor and community will be able to meet the identified needs of the refugee(s).

If the sponsorship undertaking is approved, ROC-O updates the client file in the Global Case Management System (GCMS). The migration officer completes the case, makes travel arrangements and sends a Notification of Arrival Transmission (NAT) to ROC-O at least 10 days before the refugee is travelling. The NAT will specify that the case is part of the JAS program and will include the name of the sponsor.

If the sponsor is not approved or no sponsor is found in 4 months, ROC-O contacts the migration office to discuss next steps. The refugee may be resettled under the GAR program.

Arranging for support in Canada

Many arrangements may be required in preparing the refugee for departure to Canada.

Requests for contributions

Migration officers may request a pre-arrival contribution to offset the costs associated with the transportation loan for exceptionally higher-needs JAS refugees who will be resettled to any province other than Quebec.

For information on requesting contributions for pre-arrival cases, consult the Resettlement Assistance Program (RAP) contributions section of the Immigration Loans Program (ILP) program delivery instructions.

Resettlement Assistance Program (RAP) income support and Interim Federal Health Program (IFHP) coverage duration

JAS sponsorships established before the refugee’s departure to Canada have a 24-month duration from the date of arrival in Canada. This applies to both the RAP income support and sponsor support.

Based on the information provided in the NAT, RAP officers confirm arrangements for support in Canada before the refugee arrives including

  • initiating the income support commitment period and RAP income support benefits in GCMS and its associated forms and processes (for example, updating the SAP system) to reflect the 24-month JAS sponsorship
  • ensuring that the RAP Income Support Agreement reflects the 24-month commitment of RAP benefits
  • ensuring that the Interim Federal Health Program (IFHP) coverage period reflects the 24-month commitment period

Note: The IFHP coverage period will be initiated in accordance with the JAS sponsorship period listed in GMCS (24 months). No action is required by RAP officers when refugees join the JAS program before they arrive in Canada, other than to verify in GCMS that the coverage reflects the 24-month commitment period.

Pre-arrival case conferencing

Before the refugee’s arrival, the RAP officer informs the RAP service provider organization (SPO) that a JAS refugee is arriving and helps arrange a case conferencing meeting (in person, by phone, etc.) with the RAP SPO and the sponsor to

  • review settlement information
  • complete the JAS Roles and Responsibilities Checklist (IMM 5630)

The RAP SPO sends the completed IMM 5630 to the RAP officer for review and inclusion in the refugee’s RAP-IS application. The sponsor, the RAP SPO and the refugee must also receive a copy.

Shared support obligations

JAS refugees are part of the GAR resettlement stream and are fully eligible for all RAP supports, including

  • a RAP SPO reception on arrival to Canada
  • temporary accommodation
  • RAP needs assessments and referrals
  • immediate and essential orientation and referrals to the Settlement Program and other supports

The RAP Service Provider Handbook contains more information on the role of the RAP SPO in delivering RAP services and on joint assistance sponsorships.

After the refugee arrives in Canada, the sponsoring group meets with the refugee and the RAP SPO (if applicable) and begins sharing sponsorship obligations as outlined in the JAS Roles and Responsibilities Checklist (IMM 5630).
