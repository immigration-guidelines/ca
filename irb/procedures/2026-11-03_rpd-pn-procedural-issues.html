Refugee Protection Division: Practice notice on procedural issues

Page Content

​​The practice notice on procedural issues explains how the Refugee Protection Division (RPD) applies certain procedural requirements, including transitional measures for amended RPD Rules, deadlines, applications, and procedures.

For more information, see Questions and answers: Practice notice on procedural issues.

1. Introduction

This notice was originally issued on September 9, 2024. It is being updated effective November 3, 2026, to reflect amendments to the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (IRPA) and the Refugee Protection Division Rules (RPD Rules).

2. Transitional matters

The RPD Rules have been amended, and the new requirements take effect on November 3, 2026. To provide claimants, counsel and other representatives with a reasonable period to adjust their practices and comply with the amended requirements, the RPD will apply the temporary transitional measures set out in this section.

2.1 Personal disclosure time limits

The date on which a claim is referred to the RPD determines which time limit applies to personal documents. Personal documents include documents about the claimant or the particular claim, such as identity documents, police reports, medical records, letters, photographs, and other evidence relating to the claimant's circumstances.

Claims referred before November 3, 2026

For a claim referred to the RPD on or before November 2, 2026, personal documents must be ​provided no later than 10 days before the date fixed for the hearing, unless another time limit applies or the RPD allows late disclosure.

Example: A claim was referred on October 15, 2026, and the hearing is scheduled for February 8, 2027. The claimant must provide personal documents by January 29, 2027. The new 30-day-after-referral time limit does not apply to this claim.

Claims referred from November 3, 2026, to January 3, 2027

The amended RPD Rules generally require a claimant's personal documents to be provided no later than 30 days after referral for claims referred after November 3, 2026. To give claimants, counsel and other representatives a reasonable period to adjust to this new requirement, the RPD extends the time limit to 60 days after referral for claims referred from November 3, 2026, to January 3, 2027, inclusive.​

This is an automatic extension. A claimant does not need to make an application to use the 60-day time limit.

Example: A claim is referred to the RPD on November 12, 2026. Personal documents must be received by the RPD no later than January 11, 2027, rather than December 12, 2026.

Example: A claim is referred on January 3, 2027. Personal documents must be received by the RPD no later than March 4, 2027.

Claims referred on or after January 4, 2027

For a claim referred on or after January 4, 2027, personal documents must be provided within 30 days after referral, as required by RPD Rule 34(3)(b).

Example: A claim is referred on January 10, 2027. Personal documents must be received by the RPD no later than February 9, 2027.

Documents obtained after the applicable time limit

The applicable time limit does not prevent a claimant from asking the RPD to accept a relevant personal document obtained later. A claimant who cannot provide a document within the applicable time limit should provide the documents that are available on time and should not delay the entire disclosure package while waiting for another document.

A claimant who later obtains an additional personal document must provide it without delay and apply to submit late disclosure in accordance with section 5.3 of this notice. The member will decide whether the document may be used, taking into account the factors in the RPD Rules, including its relevance and probative value, whether it is new evidence, and whether the party could have provided it on time with reasonable effort.

Example: A claimant requested a police report before the applicable time limit, but the issuing authority provided it afterward. The claimant should submit the report and the late-disclosure application as soon as it is received, explaining when it was requested, when it was received, why it could not reasonably have been provided earlier and why it is important for the claim.

2.2 Country conditions evidence

Country conditions evidence is subject to the separate disclosure time limits in the RPD Rules and is not subject to the personal-document time limits described in section 2.1. This flexibility recognizes that country conditions may change between the referral of a claim and the hearing, and allows parties to provide relevant, up-to-date evidence within the applicable hearing-based time limits (i.e., no later than 10 days before the date fixed for the hearing).

2.3 Changes or additions to the Basis of Claim form

For a claimant whose claim was referred to the RPD on or before November 2, 2026, the deadline for providing changes or additions to the Basis of Claim (BOC) form depends on when the claimant receives the notice to appear referred to in RPD Rule 25.

If the claimant receives the notice to appear on or before January 3, 2027, changes or additions to the BOC form must be received no later than 10 days before the date fixed for the hearing. If the claimant receives the notice to appear on or after January 4, 2027, the changes or additions must be received no later than 5 days after the day on which the notice to appear is received. This temporary measure is meant to give claimants, counsel and other representatives a reasonable period to adjust to this new requirement.

Example: A claimant whose claim was referred before November 3, 2026, received a notice to appear on October 15, 2026, for a hearing on December 17, 2026. The claimant may provide changes or additions to the BOC form until December 7, 2026.

2.4 Joined claims

When a claim referred to the RPD on or after November 3, 2026, is joined to a claim referred before that date, personal documents for all joined claims may be provided no later than 10 days before the date fixed for the hearing. The documents in the claim referred on or after November 3 will not be considered late despite RPD Rule 34(3)(b). This avoids different personal-disclosure time limits within the same joined proceeding.

Example: A parent's claim was referred on October 20, 2026. The child's claim was referred on November 20, 2026, and the claims are joined. Personal documents for both claims may be provided no later than 10 days before the hearing.

3. Changes to the Basis of Claim form or information

3.1 Mandatory form

A claimant who wants to change or add information to their BOC form or BOC information must use the Changes to the basis of claim (BOC) form or basis of claim information​. The claimant should identify each change clearly. The updated information and the completed form must be provided together.

The RPD will apply the requirement to use the form flexibly for a claimant who does not have counsel. An unrepresented claimant should still provide the changes in writing and identify the parts being changed.

Example: A claimant notices that a date in their narrative is incorrect. The claimant should use the required form to identify the original date and provide the correct date.

4. How to provide a document

4.1 Electronic exchange of documents preferred

The RPD prefers to receive documents electronically because electronic delivery is generally faster, safer and allows the sender to keep a record of transmission. Parties must follow the applicable filing instructions, including any requirements for file format, size, naming and proof of delivery.

A document is not considered provided merely because it was sent. The sender should retain the confirmation or other record showing when and how it was delivered.

For more information about how to submit documents electronically, consult Practice notice: Providing documents to the RPD and the RAD electronically or by fax.

4.2 Use of fax by detainees

A claimant who is detained may send documents to the RPD by fax. This is an exception to the general removal of fax as an accepted method of delivery under the RPD Rules and is intended to account for the limited filing options that may be available in detention.

4.3 Documents for the Minister

If the Minister intervenes in a refugee claim, the claimant must send the Minister a copy of every document that the claimant provides to the RPD. This applies whether the Minister participates at the hearing or intervenes only by providing written evidence or arguments.

The My Case portal delivers documents to the RPD, not to the Minister. A claimant who uses My Case must therefore send a separate copy to the Minister using an accepted method and keep proof that the copy was sent.

4.4 Translations

A document that is not in English or French must be accompanied by an English or French translation and a declaration completed by a human translator confirming that the translation is accurate, as required by the RPD Rules.

Translation software, including online tools or artificial intelligence, may assist the translator, but software output alone is not sufficient. A human translator must review the translation, provide the declaration of accuracy, and disclose in the declaration if artificial intelligence was used to assist with the translation. See Practice notice: Use of artificial intelligence in IRB proceedings for more information.

Example: A claimant uses software to prepare a draft translation of a police report. A human translator must review the complete translation, confirm its accuracy in a declaration, and state that artificial intelligence or translation software was used.

5. Applications

5.1 Change of date and time

5.1.1 Apply without delay using the RPD's form

A party asking to change the date or time of a proceeding must apply as soon as they become aware of the reason for the request. The party must use the Application to change the date or time of a proceeding and provide the information needed for the RPD to decide the request. The RPD will apply the form requirement flexibly for an unrepresented claimant or protected person.

The scheduled proceeding remains in place unless the RPD grants the application. When deciding the application, the member will consider the applicable factors in the RPD Rules, including whether the application was made without delay.

Example: A party learns two weeks before the hearing that their counsel has a serious scheduling conflict. The party should apply immediately to change the date of the hearing rather than wait until the hearing date.

5.1.2. No requirement for medical certificate

A party applying to change the date or time of a proceeding is not automatically required to provide a medical certificate or explain why one was not provided, despite RPD Rule 54.

A party may provide a medical certificate or other supporting information if it helps explain the request.

The RPD may request a medical certificate in a particular case. If the party cannot obtain one when asked to do so, they must explain the efforts made to obtain it and why it could not be provided.

5.2 Affidavits or statutory declarations with applications, responses, and replies

Evidence supporting an application, response or reply does not have to be given in an affidavit or statutory declaration, despite RPD Rules 50, 51 and 52.

A party may still provide an affidavit or statutory declaration if they consider it helpful.

The RPD may require an affidavit or statutory declaration in a particular case. If it does, the party must comply with that direction.

5.3 Procedures for late disclosure

Disclosure is late when it is provided after the time limit that applies to that document. The applicable time limit may depend on the claim's referral date, the type of document, whether the document responds to other disclosure, and the date of the hearing. A late document may not be used unless the RPD allows it.

A party who wants the RPD to accept late disclosure must provide the document and apply using the Application to submit late disclosure to the Refugee Protection Division. The RPD will apply the form requirement flexibly for an unrepresented claimant, but the claimant must still clearly ask that the document be accepted and explain why it is late.

The application should identify each late document, explain how it is relevant to the claimant's particular circumstances, and explain why it was not provided within the applicable time limit. The explanation should include when the document was requested, obtained or received, as applicable, and the steps taken to provide it without delay.

The member will decide whether the document may be used by applying the factors in the RPD Rules. Providing a document and an application does not mean that the document has been accepted.

Documents requested by the presiding member during or after a hearing are governed by the member's directions and are not late disclosure solely because they are provided after an ordinary disclosure time limit.

If disclosure is both late and over the country-conditions page limit outlined in section 5.4 below, the party must address both issues and provide both the late-disclosure application and the voluminous-disclosure application.

Example: A claimant receives a medical report after the applicable time limit. The claimant should provide the report and the late-disclosure application without delay, explaining when the report was requested and received and why it could not reasonably have been provided earlier.

5.4 Procedures for voluminous disclosure of country conditions evidence

For the purposes of disclosure, documents generally fall into 2​ categories: personal documents and country conditions evidence.

Personal documents concern the claimant or the particular claim, such as identity documents, police reports, medical records, letters and photographs. Country conditions evidence describes conditions in a country and may include human rights reports, news articles or research publications. When providing more than one document, the party must include a list identifying each document and number the pages as required by RPD Rule 31.

5.4.1 Personal documents

There is no numerical page limit for personal documents. However, every document must be relevant, must not duplicate material already provided, and must comply with the RPD Rules, including translation requirements.

Example: A claimant may provide a 120-page medical record without applying for voluminous disclosure because the record is personal evidence. The record must still be relevant and organized in accordance with the RPD Rules.

5.4.2 Country conditions evidence

In a refugee claim, a party may provide up to 100 pages of country conditions evidence per file and per country without applying for permission. The limit applies to country conditions evidence, not personal documents.

To exceed the limit, the party must use the Application to submit voluminous disclosure to the Refugee Protection Division. The RPD will apply the form requirement flexibly for an unrepresented claimant, but the claimant must still clearly request permission to exceed the limit.

The application should explain how each document is relevant to the claimant's situation. Consult the relevant National Documentation Packages (NDP) to ensure documents do not duplicate what is in the NDP.

The RPD applies the following criteria:

  • claims heard together (for example, several claimants in one family) are considered 1 file
  • when printing on both sides of a sheet, each side counts as 1 page, so a single sheet printed on both sides is counted as 2 pages
  • when a claim involves multiple countries, the page limit is 100 pages for each country
  • when a document is translated, only the original pages count (not the translated pages)
  • when a claim is returned to the RPD for redetermination by the Refugee Appeal Division or the Federal Court, a party may submit an additional 100 pages of country conditions evidence per country without making an application
  • documents sent to the RPD before October 15, 2024, do not count against the total number of pages

Example: A family whose claims are heard together has one 100-page limit for country conditions evidence for each country involved, not a separate 100-page limit for each family member.

Example: A 20-page report and its 20-page translation count as 20 pages toward the limit because only the original pages are counted.

6. Other matters

6.1 Presence of minor refugee claimants at a hearing

A minor claimant is not required to attend their refugee hearing unless the RPD directs otherwise. A minor is a person who is under 18 years of age on the date of the hearing.

A minor may attend after consulting their designated representative. The designated representative should consider the minor's circumstances and best interests when discussing attendance.

If the member requires the minor to attend, the RPD will notify the parties before the hearing. The parties should advise the RPD as early as possible of any accommodation or procedural measure the minor may need.

Example: A 15-year-old claimant does not have to attend merely because they are included in a family claim. If the member requires the minor's attendance, the RPD will notify the parties.

6.2 Members' use of electronic signatures

An RPD member may sign reasons for decision electronically. An electronic signature satisfies the signature requirement in paragraphs 68(1)(b) and 68(2)(b) of the RPD Rules.

A decision signed electronically has the same validity as a decision signed in ink. No further signature or certified paper copy is required solely because the decision was signed electronically.

6.3 Documents with graphic content

A party providing photographs, videos or other evidence that depicts violence, serious injury or sexually explicit acts must clearly label the material “Notice: Graphic Content.” The label allows members and staff to take appropriate steps before viewing the material.

The warning must be visible before the content is opened. Depending on how the material is provided, the warning should appear on the package cover, in the email subject line, in the document list or table of contents, and immediately before the graphic material.

The RPD will return graphic content that is not properly labelled. The party may resubmit it with the required warning, subject to any applicable disclosure time limit.

Example: If graphic photographs appear at pages 25 to 28 of a disclosure package, the cover or document list should contain the warning, and another warning should appear immediately before page 25.

7. Authority

Paragraph 159(1)(g) of the IRPA provides that the Chairperson has authority to take any action that may be necessary to ensure that members of the Board carry out their duties efficiently and without undue delay. The Chairperson has delegated this authority to Deputy Chairpersons.

RPD Rule 69 provides that, in the absence of a provision in the RPD Rules dealing with a matter, the Division may do whatever is necessary to deal with the matter.

RPD Rule 70 provides that the Division may, after giving notice to the parties and an opportunity to object, change a requirement of a rule, excuse a person from a requirement of a rule, and extend or shorten a time limit.
