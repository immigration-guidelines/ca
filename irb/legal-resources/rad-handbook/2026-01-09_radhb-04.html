4. Admissibility of evidence

4.1. Overview

RAD appeals are to proceed without a hearing and on the basis of the record of the proceedings of the RPD.Note 1Subsection 110(3) of the IRPA allows the RAD to accept documentary evidence and written submissions from the Minister and the person who is the subject of the appeal (person). However, the evidence that the RAD may accept from a person on appeal is limited to evidence that meets the criteria for proposed new evidence set out in subsection 110(4) of the IRPA.Note 2 The person may present only evidence that arose after the rejection of their claim or that was not reasonably available, or that the person could not reasonably have been expected in the circumstances to have presented, at the time of the rejection.

This limitation does not apply to the Minister and therefore the Minister’s evidence does not need to be new evidence that meets the criteria in subsection 110(4) of the IRPA to be admissible on appeal.

In deciding whether it can accept a person’s proposed new evidence, the RAD must be satisfied that the evidence meets one of the three criteria under subsection 110(4) of the IRPANote 3 and all three of the factors set out in Singh.Note 4 If the evidence does not meet one of the three criteria under subsection 110(4) there is no need to proceed to an assessment of the three Singh factors.Note 5 Alternatively, if the proposed new evidence does not meet one of the three Singh factors, it is open to the RAD to reject the proposed new evidence, solely on the basis of that Singh factor and the RAD need not assess the criteria in subsection 110(4) of the IRPA first.Note 6

4.1.1. New Evidence in Response to Minister’s Evidence

If the person submits evidence in response to evidence submitted by the Minister, subsection 110(5) of the IRPA provides that subsection 110(4) of the IRPA does not apply to the person’s evidence.Note 7

4.1.2. New Evidence in Response to Request for Submissions

If the person submits evidence in response to a request for submissions from the RAD, the evidence must still meet the statutory requirements and factors in Singh to be admissible.Note 8

4.1.3. New evidence at oral hearing

Subsection 171(a.1) of the IRPA sets out that subject to subsection 110(4) of the IRPA, if a hearing is held, the RAD must give the person and the Minister the opportunity to present evidence. Therefore, the criteria for the admissibility of new evidence apply to evidence presented by a person at a RAD oral hearing as well.

4.1.4. Onus and RAD Rule 3(3)(g)(iii)

The onus is on the person to show how the evidence meets the requirements of subsection 110(4) and how that evidence relates to the person.Note 9 RAD Rule 3(3)(g)(iii) requires the person to provide this information in their memorandum.

Where the explanation may be found within the proposed new evidence or in an affidavit submitted with the proposed new evidence, the RAD may err if it rejects the proposed new evidence on the sole basis that the person failed to provide full and detailed submissions concerning subsection 110(4) in their memorandum.Note 10

While a person’s affidavit, submitted with the Appellant’s record, may contain new facts and therefore potentially new evidence, it is reasonable for the RAD to treat parts of the affidavit that are in the nature of submissions as submissions and not new evidence. The RAD may also refer to an explanation found in the affidavit as another example of an inconsistency even though the affidavit was not accepted as new evidence.Note 11

The full and detailed submissions required by RAD Rule 3(3)(g)(iii) form the basis upon which the RAD decides if the criteria in subsection 110(4) are met.Note 12 In the absence of these submissions, it may be impossible for the RAD to determine whether the requirements of subsection 110(4) are met.Note 13 Submissions should clearly show how each piece of proposed new evidence meets the criteria.Note 14

However, where minimal submissions have been made on the admissibility of new evidence, the Courts have found it appropriate for the RAD to carefully consider whether, none the less, the new evidence may meet the admissibility criteria.Note 15

The explanation given for the submission of proposed new evidence may be based on an allegation of counsel incompetence which prevented the evidence from being put forward before the rejection of the claim by the RPD.Note 16 Documents submitted to the RAD in furtherance of the person’s argument of counsel incompetence, ought to be assessed under 110(4) for admissibility into evidence.Note 17 The RAD will have to consider the allegations made against counsel, including both sides of the arguments if the allegations are contested, as part of its assessment of the admissibility of proposed new evidence.Note 18

4.1.5. New evidence and reopening

Once a RAD proceeding is re-opened, the RAD appeal is de novo and the RAD exercises its discretion to determine what if any, new evidence should be admitted applying the statutory criteria in subsection 110(4) of the IRPA and the Singh factors.Note 19

4.1.6. Providing reasons

The RAD must provide reasons as to why it is accepting the proposed new evidence.Note 20 The reasons must explain how the evidence meets one of the three criteria in subsection 110(4) of the IRPA and how it satisfies all three of the Singh factors: newness, relevance, and credibility. If the RAD rejects proposed new evidence the reasons must show that the RAD considered the explanations, submissions and supporting evidence when deciding the criteria for admissibility have not been met.Note 21

4.1.7. Doctrine and case law

UNHCR Guidelines, case law and IRB decisions are not evidence and are considered doctrinal or legal support and therefore should not be treated as proposed new evidence which must meet subsection 110(4) and Singh requirements when submitted to the RAD.Note 22

4.2. Evidence from person who is the subject of the appeal

4.2.1. Admissibility of new evidence under explicit criteria of subsection 110(4)

There is no room for discretion in admitting proposed new evidence that does not meet one of the criteria in subsection 110(4) of the IRPA. The RAD must be satisfied the proposed new evidence meets one of the explicit criteria in subsection 110(4) of the IRPA.Note 23 The possibility that proposed new evidence may contradict a finding by the RPD is not sufficient to bring the evidence with the parameters of subsection 110(4).Note 24 Nor is it relevant to the analysis of the explicit criteria in subsection 110(4) that the probative value and credibility of proposed new evidence be assessed.Note 25

There is an expectation that a claimant will present to the RPD all the evidence that is available prior to the rejection of the claim. An appeal to the RAD is not an opportunity for the person to present additional evidence, previously available or reasonable to expect, to address deficiencies in their case that become clear when the claim is rejected by the RPD. New evidence is “not intended to be a tune-up procedure for the RAD.”Note 26

The onus is on the person to provide full and detailed submissions regarding how any documentary evidence the person wishes to rely on not only meets the criteria requirements of subsection 110(4), but also how that evidence relates to the appellant.Note 27

Pursuant to subsection 110(4) of the IRPA, the person may only present evidence which meets one of the following criteria.

4.2.1.1. The evidence arose after the rejection of the claim

The date of the document being submitted is not necessarily determinative of whether the evidence arose after the rejection of the claim; one also considers the date of the events referred to within the document.Note 28

4.2.1.2. The evidence was not reasonably available at the time of the rejection of the claim

The person must show that the evidence could not, with reasonable diligence, have been identified and disclosed prior to the rejection of the claim.Note 29 If a concern became evident during the RPD hearing there is an exception that evidence addressing the concern will be provided, if possible, immediately after the hearing.Note 30 An argument that the proposed new evidence responds to a credibility issue that arose in the RPD decision is insufficient if the issue was raised at the hearing.Note 31 In deciding whether the evidence was or was not reasonably available, the RAD must look at other relevant evidence, such as any accompanying affidavit from the person, and not just the proposed new evidence itself, for the explanation proffered.Note 32 Absent an explanation for a delay in presenting evidence that otherwise could have been presented before the rejection, the RAD may find the evidence does not meet subsection 110(4).Note 33 The RAD may also assess the reasonableness of the explanation offered for the delay in presenting the evidence and take into account whether a request for an adjournment was made at the RPD in the appropriate circumstances.Note 34

4.2.1.3. The person could not reasonably have been expected in the circumstances to have presented the evidence at the time of the rejection of the claim

Evidence that is available earlier may not become relevant until later in the process in ways that the person may not initially expect.Note 35 However, the issue of IFA is one that claimants should always anticipate even if not identified before the RPD hearing.Note 36 Being surprised by the outcome of the RPD hearing does not necessarily entitle the person to submit new evidence.Note 37 The fact that the person was represented by counsel at the RPD can be a factor the RAD takes into account in assessing the person’s contention that he could not have been expected to present all pertinent evidence before the time of rejection.Note 38 Conversely, the fact that a person was self-represented at the RPD does not give the RAD broad discretion to admit new evidence if conditions in 110(4) have not been met.Note 39

4.2.2. Admissibility of new evidence under the Singh factors

In addition to meeting one of the three criteria in subsection 110(4) of the IRPA, the proposed new evidence must also meet all three of the factors affirmed by the Federal Court of Appeal in Singh; newness, relevance and credibility.Note 40 If the evidence meets all of the Singh factors it is admissible. If it fails to meet one of them, it is inadmissible.Note 41 The onus is on the person to establish that these factors are met on a balance of probabilities.Note 42

The Federal Court of Appeal in Singh reviewed the implicit factors set out in the Raza decision for the admissibility of new evidence under subsection 113(a) of the IRPANote 43 and adapted them for the RAD’s application under subsection 110(4) of the IRPA. The Court affirmed that proposed new evidence must meet the newness, relevance, and credibility implicit factors for admissibility at RAD, but not the materiality factor, which is applicable only to subsection 110(6) of the IRPA.Note 44

4.2.2.1. Newness

This Raza factor, adopted in Singh, asks if the evidence is new in the sense that it is capable of:

  1. proving the current state of affairs in the country of removal or an event that occurred or a circumstance that arose after the hearing in the RPD; or
  2. proving a fact that was unknown to the refugee claimant at the time of the RPD hearing; or
  3. contradicting a finding of fact made by the RPD (including a credibility finding)Note 45

As previously discussed in section 4.2.1.1 of this chapter, a document’s newness is not determined solely by the date on which that document was authored; what is important is the date of the event or circumstance that the document seeks to prove.Note 46 When the evidence within a document is mixed, in the sense that some is new and some is repetitive of that found on the Record, the document will meet the newness factor if the new portion, for example, proves a current state of affairs or contradicts a central finding of fact by the RPD.Note 47 It is worth noting that the newness component of Singh is somewhat redundant and does not really add to the explicit requirements of subsection 110(4) of the IRPA.Note 48

4.2.2.2. Relevance

This Raza factor, adopted in Singh, asks if the evidence is relevant in the sense that it is capable of proving or disproving a fact that is relevant to the claim for protection. In the context of a RAD appeal, it has been interpreted to mean whether the proposed new evidence is relevant to determinative issues in the appeal at the RAD.Note 49 The person should explain in the memorandum how the proposed new evidence is relevant to the appeal.Note 50 RAD Rule 3(3)(g)(iii) requires the person to explain how the evidence relates to them and a lack of explanation may lead to the evidence being dismissed as irrelevant.Note 51 However, where the proposed new evidence is clearly related to allegations that are central elements in the refugee claim, the RAD must address why the new evidence would be incapable of proving or disproving the allegation.Note 52 For example, where credibility was an issue at the RPD, a psychological report may not be relevant if it does not provide any specific information on how the person’s condition could have affected his testimony.Note 53

4.2.2.3. Credibility

This Raza factor, adopted in Singh, asks if the evidence is credible considering its source and the circumstances in which it came into existence. Credibility may be assessed by an examination of the form and content of the proposed new evidence, by comparing the content of the proposed new evidence to other evidence on the record, and by sufficient evidence to establish credibly how the evidence came into being.Note 54 The source of a document cannot be found not credible solely on the basis that the person who asked for the document was found to be generally not credible.Note 55 Suspicious or fortuitous timing with respect to the production of the proposed new evidence may, in limited circumstances, support a finding that it is not credible.Note 56

The RAD is not required to hold a hearing to assess the credibility of proposed new evidence. It is when otherwise credible admitted evidence raises a serious issue with respect to the general credibility of the person that a hearing may be held.Note 57 Similarly, the RAD is not necessarily required to give notice to the person that the credibility of proposed new evidence is at issue and to provide them with an opportunity to respond to the RAD’s concerns about the evidence.Note 58 A credibility finding on the admissibility of new evidence is not equivalent to a credibility assessment of the person.Note 59 The RAD may admit new evidence that meets the “source and circumstance” credibility threshold and still give it little or no weight when assessing its credibility and probative value within the context of all the evidence in deciding the appeal.Note 60

4.2.3. Submitting evidence with the record

Proposed new evidence must be submitted by the person with the appellant’s record or reply record (or in the case of a Minister’s appeal, with the respondent’s record). RAD Rule 3(3)(d)(i) sets out that the person must within the appellant’s record provide the RAD with a written statement indicating whether the appellant is relying on any evidence referred to in subsection 110(4) of the IRPA. RAD Rule 3(3)(g)(iii) requires full and detailed submissions in the appellant’s memorandum indicating how the proposed new evidence meets the requirements of subsection 110(4). See Chapter 1: Introduction to the RAD for a detailed discussion of the rules on submitting new evidence in the respondent’s record or the reply record.

The full and detailed submissions required by RAD Rule 3(3)(g)(iii) form the basis upon which the RAD decides if the criteria in subsection 110(4) are met.Note 61 In the absence of these submissions, it may be impossible for the RAD to determine whether the requirements of subsection 110(4) are met.Note 62 Where there is an explanation provided for the lateness of the proposed new evidence in an affidavit submitted with the proposed new evidence, the RAD may err if it rejects the proposed new evidence on the sole basis that the person failed to provide full and detailed submissions concerning subsection 110(4) in their memorandum.Note 63

The explanation given for the submission of proposed new evidence may be based on an allegation of counsel incompetence at the RPD hearing.Note 64 The RAD will have to consider, as part of its assessment of the admissibility of the evidence, the allegations made against counsel and consider both sides of the arguments if the allegations are contested.Note 65

4.2.4. Submitting evidence post-record

If the proposed new evidence was not submitted with the appellant’s record or reply record (or in the case of a Minister’s appeal, with the respondent’s record), the requirements of RAD Rule 29 must first be met before the evidence may be assessed for admissibility under subsection 110(4). RAD Rule 29 sets out the procedure to be followed. In order for the person to use the proposed new evidence RAD Rule 29 (2) requires that an application be made in accordance with RAD Rule 37.Note 66RAD Rule 29(3) requires the person to include in the application an explanation of how the documents meet the requirements of subsection 110(4). If the documents are being presented by the person in response to evidence presented by the Minister, the evidence does not have to meet the criteria in subsection 110(4).Note 67

Additionally, RAD Rule 29(4) sets out the factors which the RAD must consider in deciding whether to allow the application; the document’s relevance and probative value; any new evidence the document brings to the appeal; and whether the person, with reasonable effort, could have provided the document or written submissions with the relevant record. While the list of factors enumerated in RAD Rule 29(4) is not exhaustive, the use of the word “including” before the list of factors indicates the intent that each of the three factors must be considered by the RAD in deciding whether to allow the application.Note 68 The RAD must consider the relevance and probative value of a document even when it is not satisfied as to why the document was not submitted at the time of perfection.Note 69 A failure to do so will render the RAD’s decision unreasonable.Note 70 If the proposed new evidence meets the requirements of Rule 29, the RAD then proceeds to assess the admissibility of the evidence under subsection 110(4) and the Singh factors.

4.2.5. Evidence not admitted by Refugee Protection Division

Evidence submitted to the RPD during or after the hearing but which is not accepted by the RPD does not become part of the RPD Record.Note 71 If a person wishes to rely upon such evidence on appeal, the person must follow RAD Rule 3(3)(c), which allows an appellant to include in the appellant’s record any documents that the RPD refused to accept as evidence which the appellant now wishes to rely upon in the appeal. The RAD must consider the reasons the documents were rejected for admission by the RPD and decide if the RPD erred in refusing to admit the documents.Note 72 These documents are not proposed new evidence and do not have to meet the criteria in subsection 110(4) of the IRPA and the Singh factors.Note 73

4.3. Minister's evidence

4.3.1. Disclosure of Minister's evidence

Pursuant to the RAD Rules, when the Minister intervenes in an appeal, they must show they have provided, first to the person and then to the RAD, a written notice of intervention together with any documentary evidence they wish to rely upon.Note 74 The notice of intervention must indicate the relevance of any documentary evidence provided under subsection 110(3) of the IRPA that the Minister wishes to rely upon (i.e. evidence that is not contained in the RPD record).Note 75

There are similar requirements pursuant to the RAD Rules when the Minister is the appellant. To perfect an appeal, the Minister must show they have provided, first to the person and then to the RAD, any supporting documents they wish to rely upon.Note 76 The appellant’s record must include a written statement indicating the relevance of any documentary evidence they have provided under subsection 110(3) of the IRPA.Note 77 It must also include any documents the RPD refused to accept as evidence, during or after the RPD hearing, if the Minister wants to rely on those documents in the appeal.Note 78

However, the above requirements do not preclude the Minister from submitting new evidence at a later stage in a proceeding. To the contrary, paragraph 171(a.5) of the IRPA provides that the Minister may submit further documentary evidence and written submissions at any time before the RAD makes a decision.Note 79 In all cases, the Minister must show they have provided any further documentary evidence or submissions first to the person and then to the RAD.Note 80 As the Minister can submit evidence at any time before the RAD makes a decision, the requirements of RAD Rules 29(1) through (4) do not apply.

4.3.2. Limitations on the admissibility of Minister's evidence

Further documentary evidence provided by the Minister under subsection 110(3) of the IRPA is not required to satisfy any of the admissibility criteria set out in subsection 110(4), as those criteria are expressly limited in application to the person.Note 81 However, as is the case with all evidence, the Minister’s new documentary evidence must be considered credible or trustworthy in the circumstances in order for the RAD to receive and base a decision upon it.Note 82

Like the person, the Minister may submit further evidence in response to a request by the RAD (e.g., where the RAD wishes to raise a new issue and provides the parties with an opportunity to respond). Again, such evidence is not subject to the criteria for admissibility set out in subsection 110(4) of the IRPA or RAD Rules 29(1) through (4).

4.4. Evidence provided during an oral hearing before the Refugee Appeal Division

The RAD may convene an oral hearing in certain circumstances where it has admitted new documentary evidence that was provided by the person or the Minister.Note 83 As clarified by paragraph 171(a.1) of the IRPA, documentary evidence provided by the person during an oral hearing is still subject to the requirements of subsection 110(4) of the IRPA unless it was provided in response to evidence presented by the Minister.Note 84 Furthermore, a person seeking to present documentary evidence after an appeal is perfected will be required to bring an application under RAD Rule 29(2) of the RAD Rules. Note that, as discussed in section 4.3.2 above, documentary evidence provided by the Minister is not subject to the requirements of either subsection 110(4) of the IRPA or RAD Rules 29(1) through (4).

  • Credibility: Is the evidence credible, considering its source and the circumstances in which it came into existence? If not, the evidence need not be considered.

Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration), 2021 FC 556 (CanLII), the RAD admitted evidence because it met the threshold of “source and circumstance” in Singh, but still gave it low weight in determining the merits of the appeal. At paragraph 6 the Court stated that, “there is nothing inherently illogical or inconsistent when a decision-maker admits new evidence because of its potential to affect the outcome of a case but ultimately finds it to be unreliable when examined closely against other received evidence.” Similarly in Tan v. Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration), 2021 FC 1204, paragraphs 35–36 (CanLII), the proposed new evidence was a letter from the applicant’s father which was vague and also suspicious as to its timing (in that the events referred to in the letter happened at the same time the applicant asked to submit additional evidence). The RAD found the evidence credible enough to meet the Singh requirement but gave it no weight. The Court stated at paragraph 36 that, “A document may be reliable and trustworthy to warrant admission, and yet incapable of proving the facts for which it is tendered due to a lack of probative value of its contents.” See also Tahir v. Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration), 2021 FC 1202 (CanLII), where the RAD refused to admit into evidence a warrant and summons because they did not meet the Singh credibility requirement. The Court stated that the RAD’s authenticity concerns about the warrant and the summons did not trigger a requirement to hold an oral hearing. However, in Gbemudu v. Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration), 2018 FC 451 (CanLII), the Court found the RAD erred when it did not admit the applicant’s brother’s affidavit on the basis of internal credibility problems and noted that some of the problems with the new evidence might have been resolved by giving the applicant an opportunity to address the credibility concerns.

Note 61

In Agazuma v. Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration), 2021 FC 696, paragraph 27 (CanLII), the Court notes that the applicants have the onus to establish the satisfaction of the subsection 110(4) criteria on a balance of probabilities. It was reasonable for the RAD to have found that the submissions were not full and detailed as the applicants did not specify the evidence to which their arguments pertained. In Aldana Burgos v. Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration) paragraph 21, the RAD pointed to the lack of submissions pertaining to the source and circumstances of WhatsApp messages as part of the reasons for refusing to admit the evidence. The Court found this to be reasonable. See also Tiodunmo v. Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration), 2019 FC 1489, paragraph 15 (CanLII), where the applicants failed to provide any substantive submissions on how the evidence meets subsection 110(4) or how it relates to the person. In Abdulrahman v. Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration), 2019 FC 821, paragraphs 11–12 (CanLII), there was no information in the legal assistant’s affidavit explaining how or why the documents listed in the affidavit were submitted to the RAD as new evidence and there was also no affidavit from the applicant explaining why the documents were being submitted as new evidence. The Court notes it is incumbent on the applicant to comply with Refugee Appeal Division Rules, SOR/2012-257, rule 3(3)(g)(iii) to make full and detailed submission regarding how proposed new evidence meets the requirements of subsection 110(4) and how the evidence relates to the person.
