Humanitarian and compassionate (H&C) considerations: Stage 1 processing in Canada - Consecutive or concurrent H&C applications or requests

This section contains policy, procedures and guidance used by IRCC staff. It is posted on the department’s website as a courtesy to stakeholders.

Consecutive H&C applications or requests

If an applicant had a humanitarian and compassionate (H&C) assessment and submits a new application, information and findings from any previous H&C application or request may be taken into account. Decision-makers must also consider any new information submitted with the most recent application.

H&C consideration, under subsection A25(1) and section A25.1 of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (the Act), provides the flexibility to grant permanent resident status to certain foreign nationals who would otherwise not qualify in any class, if there are sufficient H&C grounds. Applicants may make submissions on any factors affecting their personal circumstances that they believe are relevant to their application or request for H&C consideration.

H&C program objectives

Discretion is a valuable element of Canada’s immigration program. It benefits our clients and is consistent with the objectives of the the Act. The purpose of H&C discretion is to allow flexibility to approve deserving cases not covered by the legislation. Use of this discretion should not be seen as conflicting with other parts of the Act or the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations (the Regulations), but rather as a complementary provision enhancing the attainment of the objectives of the Act. The H&C decision-making process is a discretionary one that considers whether a special grant of an exemption from a requirement of the Act is warranted. Invoking subsection A25(1) and section A25.1 is an exceptional measure and not simply an alternate means of applying for permanent resident status in Canada.

In the spirit of the law, if the applicant qualifies for another class, the H&C application would be put into abeyance.

If an H&C applicant has a pending application for permanent residence in another category (e.g. protected person, spouse or common-law partner in Canada), the application that was received first normally takes precedence, although certain types of cases may have priority (e.g. spousal application). Processing of the H&C application should not begin until a decision is made on the first application. It stands to reason that when an application is submitted under one of the regular classes, the H&C application should be held off until the other application is resolved (unless the H&C application is close to completion). At the same time, it is recognized that there is nothing in the legislation or regulations to stop decision-makers from concurrently processing both applications.

Offices should continue to hold H&C applications in abeyance until the applicant obtains permanent residence status, or a final decision is made on the permanent residence application received before the H&C application.

Scenarios in which the H&C application is not examined and returned to the applicant

When an H&C application is received at intake for:

  • a protected person who has not yet submitted an application for permanent residence in the protected persons in-Canada category (PPIC) category, the H&C application will not be examined, and the application and fees will be returned
  • a protected person who has applied for PPIC status, the H&C application will not be examined, and the application and fees will be returned

Scenarios in which the H&C application can be held in abeyance

Concurrent pending applications – H&C and PPIC

When an H&C application is at either Stage 1 or Stage 2 and the applicant is a protected person who has not yet submitted an application for permanent residence in the PPIC category, decision-makers will:

  • convert the H&C application to a PPIC application
  • consider inadmissibility exemption requests in the context of the converted PPIC application; if the exemption has already been granted, it remains valid in the context of the PPIC application
  • inform the applicant of the conversion

When an H&C application is at either Stage 1 or Stage 2 and the applicant is a protected person who has an outstanding PPIC application, decision-makers will:

  • hold the H&C application in abeyance until the applicant obtains permanent resident status or the application is finalized
  • enter notes in GCMS to indicate that the H&C application is held in abeyance
  • refuse the H&C application as per standard processing when an applicant becomes a permanent resident (PR), or resume processing of the outstanding H&C application according to standard procedures, if the final decision on the PPIC application is negative

Other considerations for protected persons who submit an H&C application:

  • Protected persons applying for permanent residence under any class retain their protected person status and all protected person exemptions. None of the following inadmissibility provisions apply:
    • financial inadmissibility [section A39]
    • medical inadmissibility based on excessive demand [paragraph A38(1)(c)]
    • criminality [subsection A36(2)]
    • inadmissibility based on prior misrepresentation [paragraph A40(1)(a)]
    • inadmissibility on the basis of an inadmissible family member [section A42]
  • Protected persons may provide alternative identity documents (i.e. statutory declarations [section R178])
  • Protected persons are exempt from the Right of Permanent Residence Fee [paragraph R303(2)(c)]

Concurrent pending applications – H&C and SCLPC

When an H&C application is at either Stage 1 or Stage 2 and the applicant has an outstanding application for permanent residence under the spouse or common-law partner in Canada (SCLPC) class, decision-makers will:

  • hold the H&C application in abeyance until the applicant obtains permanent resident status or the application is finalized
  • enter notes in GCMS indicating that the H&C application is being held in abeyance
  • refuse the H&C application as per standard processing when an applicant becomes a permanent resident (PR), or
  • resume processing of the outstanding H&C application as per standard procedures, if the final decision on the SCLPC application is negative

Note: It is assumed in the scenario above that the H&C application has not been requested within the SCLPC application, as this would make it a concurrent H&C request, which is not permitted if the H&C request was received on or after June 29, 2010.

Concurrent applications for H&C and PRRA

If an applicant submits both an H&C application and a pre-removal risk assessment (PRRA) application, both assessments may be done concurrently.

Assessment of applications for H&C versus PRRA

An H&C assessment is lower in threshold than a PRRA For an H&C application, decision-makers must assess all elements of the application, except sections A96 and A97, and decide whether there are sufficient grounds to justify granting an exemption on H&C grounds.
